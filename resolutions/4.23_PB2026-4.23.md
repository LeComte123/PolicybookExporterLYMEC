(Passed Autumn 2024\)

Noting that:

* The Draghi-report, published on the 9th of September 2024, by the Italian economist, politician and former ECB chief Mario Draghi is a warning sign for the European economy.

* Draghi's report addresses the decreased European competitiveness and urges the EU to foster more investments, cut bureaucracy and finalise the internal market to increase European productivity.

* Parts of the Draghi-report's proposals have already been adopted by European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen for the work programme of her 2024–2029 Commission term.

* Draghi demands a stronger integration of the national capital-markets to ease the access to capital.

* Draghi quantifies the additional annual investment needs at over €800 billion, or about 5 percent of EU GDP.

* Considering that:

* Europe is falling far behind the American and Chinese economies.

* The relatively slower productivity growth in Europe compared to the United States has led to significant concerns about the viability of Europe’s economic approach.

* According to Draghi, the main reason for the increasing productivity gap is the fragmented and nationalised economic structure of the EU.

* Some foreign States like the People's Republic of China are systematically abusing the openness of the European market and the framework provided by the World Trade Organization (WTO) by flooding the European and the world market with highly subsidised products. At the same time, the PRC shields their domestic market from any foreign competition.

Believing that:

* It is a worrying trend that authoritarian states are the ones dominating the global economy.

* If the EU fails to catch up with its rivals, it will face 'slow agony'.

* There is insufficient investment in Europe, both public and private.

* Liberal democracies should enhance their economic cooperation with other liberal democracies.

* European countries must ensure that their trade patterns promote freedom, democracy and human rights worldwide.

* Considering the increasingly unstable world and unpredictable super powers, like the US, the European economy needs to become more independent.

* The EU needs far more coordinated industrial policy, more rapid decisions and massive investment if it wants to keep pace economically with rivals such as the United States and China.

* Decarbonization can boost competitiveness if well-managed but risks undermining it if it is poorly executed.

Lymec calls for:

* Closing the innovation gap with the United States, harmonising decarbonization with competitiveness, and enhancing economic security by reducing dependencies to be defining goals of the EU.

* Greater integration of the European economies.

* Increased investments in boosting the European economy.

* Fostering a more innovation-friendly regulatory ecosystem.

* Renewing the EU competition law, and getting more knowledge about the potential negative consequences of certain European regulations, such as the European Union merger law.

* Adapting a more growth and innovation based posture when it comes to EU merger control, while not resorting to the failed industrial policies of picking winners based on political reasonings.

* Security, democracy and human rights to define which countries to enter trade agreements with. In other words: economic policies that promote freedom.

* More institutionalised economic cooperation between liberal democracies to secure independence from authoritarian regimes.

* European-level industrial policies in clean technologies and electric vehicles.

* Developing European capital markets and completing the Banking Union, along with increasing the financing volume of the European Investment Bank (EIB).
