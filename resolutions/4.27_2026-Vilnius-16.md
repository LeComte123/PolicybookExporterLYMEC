Noting that:

- Rising energy costs and limited renewable energy production are increasing the cost of living and therefore place a financial burden on households.
- Transportation and housing costs are increasing, with limited affordable and sustainable options available to citizens.
- High taxes on food, restrictive policies in agriculture, and market barriers impact food affordability and access to innovative products.
- Homelessness remains a pressing issue in Europe.
- Rural areas suffer from limited infrastructure and connectivity, reducing their attractiveness.
- Current recycling practices and waste management lack efficiency and sustainability.
- Labour mobility and fair competition are hindered by regulatory and tax barriers across the EU.

Believing that:

- Private investments in renewable energy, housing, and sustainable infrastructure are essential to reduce costs and ensure a high standard of living.
- Supporting affordable transportation and standardized housing policies can help alleviate costs and promote fair access across Europe.
- Enhancing recycling, food policy, and flexible labour conditions can create a resilient and sustainable economic environment.
- Reducing bureaucratic hurdles and market barriers will facilitate access to affordable goods, services, and housing while promoting innovation.

LYMEC calls for:

Affordable and Sustainable Housing Solutions

- Adopting the Housing First approach as seen in Finland to address homelessness, alongside year-round emergency programs and digitized official processes.
- Supporting modular construction with standardized permits to accelerate building processes and reduce costs.
- Implementing a rent-to-own model for newly built housing on public land
- Rejecting rent caps and providing incentives, such as a real estate transfer tax allowance for first-time owner-occupied properties set relative to regional property prices.
- Encouraging modular and flexible-use buildings that can adapt over time (e.g., from kindergarten to school or health center) to reduce long-term infrastructure costs.
- Encouraging Member States and local authorities to liberalise restrictive zoning and planning regulations, facilitate higher-density and mixed-use developments, and allow the conversion and subdivision of existing buildings into housing, particularly in areas well connected to public transport.

Secure and Market-Based Energy System

- Establishing a European Energy Union, and ensuring transnational grid stability.
- Introducing regional electricity price zones so consumers benefit directly from locally produced cheap renewable energy
- Enabling citizen participation in local renewable energy projects
- Abolishing electricity taxes and energy-related levies at EU level and reduce VAT on electricity.
- Strengthening and expanding the European Emissions Trading System (ETS) as the central market-based instrument for decarbonisation.
- Returning a fixed, equal per-household climate dividend from European Emissions Trading System (ETS) revenues to directly reduce household energy costs and ensure that decarbonisation remains socially fair.

Affordable and Accessible Transportation

- Supporting member states in introducing flat-rate public transport tickets to encourage public transit over personal vehicles.
- Enhancing cross-border railway infrastructure and establishing an EU-wide high-speed rail network
- Expanding the validity of national and regional flat-rate public transport tickets to regional services within cross-border regions, on a reciprocal basis between neighbouring states, and providing open timetable and fare data so that cross-border journeys can be planned and booked in one place.
- Enabling the deployment of autonomous on-demand public transport services by establishing a harmonised EU regulatory framework for self-driving vehicles and supporting pilot programmes in rural and underserved areas.

Rural Development and Connectivity

- Expanding 5G and broadband infrastructure in rural areas to improve internet access, creating a foundation for economic development and connectivity.
- Strengthening competition in mobile telecommunications by ensuring fair wholesale access for mobile virtual network operators (MVNOs) across all member states, enabling new market entrants to offer innovative services at competitive prices.
- Park-and-ride facilities should be built at the edges of metropolitan regions and cities, combining affordable parking with convenient access points to local public transport services such as metro lines, and bus networks.

Improved Waste Management and Recycling Standards

- Harmonizing the European deposit system and investing in materials research to improve recyclability and reusability of products.
- Strengthening global plastic agreements, and improving penalties for illegal waste disposal in oceans.
- Removing legal barriers for using recycled materials in housing construction, such as recycled concrete, linking waste reduction with sustainable building practices.

Enhanced Food and Agriculture Policies

- Simplifying regulatory approval for GMOs and promoting agriculture based on consumer safety, lowering food production costs.
- Streamlining regulatory approval for cultivated in-vitro meat and promoting its market access based on consumer safety, expanding food choice and lowering long-term production costs.
- Reducing regulatory barriers for agricultural trade to promote lower food prices through competition and open markets.
- Cutting VAT on essential food items to lower food costs directly.
- Removing market barriers for plant-based alternatives by rejecting naming restrictions on terms like "burger" or "sausage", ending VAT discrimination against plant-based dairy substitutes, and ensuring technology-neutral food regulations that let consumers choose freely.

Flexible and Open Labour Market

- Promoting flexible working conditions, enabling work and study mobility across Europe.
- Removing regulatory barriers, including the EU Minimum Wage Directive and the EU Working Time Directive, to increase cross-border labour flexibility.
- Reducing tax burdens and bureaucratic obstacles, increasing net income for individuals.
- Enabling remote work across borders through digital infrastructure and harmonised rules, allowing young people to access higher-paying jobs without relocating to expensive cities.
- Promoting gradual and flexible retirement models while strengthening financially sustainable, pre-funded pension systems and reducing reliance on systems where younger generations finance current pensions.
- Promoting flexible childcare models, including registered childminders and employer-supported arrangements, by establishing EU-wide quality standards that focus on outcomes rather than prescriptive staff ratios.
