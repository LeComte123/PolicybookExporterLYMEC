(Former 9.37 A New Chapter for EU-UK Relations, prior to Warsaw, Autumn 2024\)

**Resetting the EU-UK Relationship.**

Cosignatory MOs: Alliance Youth, Ógra Fianna Fáil, Unge Venstre, CUF, Adam Volk (IMS), Silvia Fernandez (IMS), JuLis, JNC, LHG, Jonge Democraten, Radikal Ungdom, Young Progressive Slovakia, Centerstudenter, JUNOS, ERP Youth, Venstre Ungdom, JOVD

*This motion archives 9.37 A New Chapter for EU-UK Relations*

Noting that:

* The best relationship between the United Kingdom and the European Union is one in which the United Kingdom is a full member of the European Union;

* In the process of leaving the European Union, the United Kingdom also ceased to be a full member of many key European schemes and institutions;

* From next year, the ETIAS travel authorisation system will come into effect for travellers from the United Kingdom. Erecting further barriers between the United Kingdom and the European Union;

* The United Kingdom and the European Union have since entered into a Withdrawal Agreement, a Trade and Cooperation Agreement, and the Northern Ireland Protocol, designed to prevent a hard border on the island of Ireland and to protect the EU’s internal market;

* The Windsor Framework was agreed by the United Kingdom and the European Union and was formally adopted in March 2023;

* The Trade and Cooperation Agreement is due to be reviewed in 2026;

* The previous government of the United Kingdom made attempts to pass legislation to override the provision of international law, to avoid its obligations under agreements with the European Union, and made further overtures towards leaving other key European institutions such as the European Convention on Human Rights;

* The new UK Government, led by Keir Starmer’s Labour Party, has stated it intends to ‘reset’ the United Kingdom’s relationship with the European Union. However, the exact details of this are still to be clarified in the public sphere;

* Denmark, Luxembourg, Spain, and Portugal have reached reciprocal agreements with the UK to allow their citizens the right to vote in local elections in each other’s countries.

Understanding that:

* The path to renegotiating the United Kingdom’s membership of the European Union is a long one that requires a sustained effort at building trust between the people and governments of the United Kingdom and the European Union;

* Recent polling shows that a clear majority of the United Kingdom’s population believe Brexit has gone badly, with a recent YouGov poll showing 25% of people believe Brexit has gone ‘fairly badly’, whilst an additional 34% believe Brexit has gone ‘very badly’;

* Yougov polling from January also shows that a majority of the United Kingdom’s population supports rejoining the EU, with 33% ‘strongly supporting’ the idea;

* There is a risk that the introduction of ETIAS for UK travellers may undermine the Common Travel Area, and has the potential to create further difficulties for asylum seekers living in Northern Ireland;

* Many non-EU states continue to participate in EU-affiliated peripheral schemes;

* The European Union has extended offers to negotiate with the United Kingdom on key EU-affiliated schemes, most notably Erasmus+;

* The vast majority of people in Northern Ireland are entitled to hold Irish citizenship by birthright.

Believing that:

* The United Kingdom’s relationship with the European Union is far from a settled matter, with many issues important to young people still left to be resolved;

* Closer cooperation between the European Union and the United Kingdom has several tangible benefits on key areas such as security, energy policy, climate change, and international trade;

* The Erasmus+ Programme offers unparalleled opportunities for young people, championing education and skills, social mobility, and global cooperation, and that the United Kingdom has benefited both economically and academically from this reciprocal agreement;

* The European Medicines Agency offers invaluable cooperation on medicines as well as research and innovation in the pharmaceutical sector;

* Membership of Europol is in the United Kingdom’s best interests in order to promote safety, security, and fight against organised and cross-border crime;

* Euratom remains a vital structure for energy security in Europe;

* The European Aviation Safety Agency plays a vital role in keeping aviation regulations standardised across Europe, ensuring safety and piece of mind for passengers;

* In all of these schemes, full membership of the European Union is not required;

* Protecting citizens’ rights and liberties must remain a strong priority in the developing relationship between the United Kingdom and the European Union;

* EU residents living in the UK and UK residents living in the EU should have a democratic voice in local governance.

LYMEC Calls For:

* Negotiations for the United Kingdom to re-enter Erasmus+ to begin immediately, with the aim of the United Kingdom returning to Erasmus+ as an associated country by 2027;

* Any agreement on Erasmus+ must treat European students in an equitable manner, both in regards to fees and residency rights for the period of their study;

* The European Union and the United Kingdom to negotiate passport- and visa-free school and/ or educational trips between the United Kingdom and EU member states, on a reciprocal basis including simplified customs and travel procedures for school groups, and a focus on fostering cross-cultural understanding and educational enrichment;

* Discussions to take place between the European Union and the United Kingdom with the goal of reaching a reciprocal exemption from electronic travel authorisation schemes. Aiming to eliminate costs and reduce border delays for holiday-makers, while maintaining high standards for border security and data protection;

* Further negotiations to take place on other key schemes of importance to both the European Union and the United Kingdom, especially the European Medicines Agency, Euratom, and the European Aviation Safety Agency;

* The European Union and the United Kingdom to negotiate renewed and enhanced cooperation with Europol, enabling the UK to participate in joint investigations, intelligence sharing, and cross-border crime prevention. This cooperation should ensure that both EU and UK authorities can work together more effectively to address organized crime, cyber threats, and terrorism;

* Revision of the Trade and Cooperation Agreement to extend and simplify cooperation in areas such as trade, energy and security between the United Kingdom and the European Union for the benefit of our continent;

* The United Kingdom and the European Union to consider the mutual value of a staged approach to the United Kingdom rejoining the single market, and eventually the Union, to build and sustain permanent trust between the United Kingdom and the European Union

* The continued protection and promotion of citizens’ rights and liberties, including political rights and workers’ rights, in both the United Kingdom and the European Union;

* Members of LYMEC and ALDE to support work and develop initiatives at local and national levels to improve relationships between the United Kingdom and the European Union.

* A reciprocal agreement to negotiate voting rights in local elections at the EU level.
