(Passed Autumn 2024\)

Submitted by: Silvia Fernández (IMS), Adam Volf (IMS)

Noting that:

* Housing costs and rents have been steadily increasing across Europe over the past decade, with a significant acceleration during the pandemic. This has been exacerbated by a decline in construction and renovation activities, leading to a shortage of affordable housing in many European countries.

* Accelerating urbanisation and demographic shift has further fueled the demand for housing in certain areas, where supply has not kept up.

* The combination of soaring energy prices and the cost of living crisis has made housing less affordable. There is a shortage of social housing to meet the increased demand, which is affecting low and middle-income households.

* Young people, especially in urban areas and larger cities, are particularly impacted by rising housing costs. Many struggle to afford decent housing due to high rents and property prices combined with low incomes and/or high taxes in different Member States.

* In the EU, nearly 28% of young people (aged 15-29 years) live in overcrowded housing, with this percentage having increased in recent years. Romania and Poland have some of the most severe housing congestion.

* The lack of affordable housing leads to housing insecurity, with many young people either moving back in with their parents (referred to as "boomerang youth") or taking longer to leave their parental homes.

* In Spain and Italy, young people typically leave their parents' home at the age of 30 or older. In large cities where housing costs are particularly high rent is often too high to afford on a single income, forcing young people to share accommodation with friends or their partners.

* Unaffordable housing affects the well-being and social inclusion of young people, making it difficult for them to establish independent lives.

* Local housing markets across Europe are fundamentally different and difficult to compare. Variations in fiscal policies and the supply of social housing are just some of the many differences.

* Believing that:

* In much of Europe, young people are forced to spend too high a portion of their income on basic living expenses such as housing, food, and utilities.

* Many young people balance part-time jobs with lower wages, or have less employment stability, which makes it even more challenging to cover living expenses.

* The cost of living crisis adds financial pressure, making it difficult for young people to save money or invest in their future.

* Income control and rental caps such as rent control result in a decrease in supply and an increase in discrimination. In the longer term this form of policy will also lead to decreased development due to increased uncertainty and decreased trust in the government.

LYMEC calls for:

* The recently appointed Housing Commissioner to prioritise addressing housing issues and ensuring that housing remains a top priority on the political agenda.

* A commitment from all EU Member States and the EU Commission to the EU Affordable Housing Plan. This includes: (1) a commitment to revise state aid rules to enable housing support measures and energy efficiency renovations; and (2) a commitment to collect and provide accurate, high\-quality and comparable data on housing markets, and to monitor housing affordability across the EU, including at local and regional level.

* The development of operational toolkits to assist local authorities and other stakeholders in effectively utilising EU funds for social housing investments.

* Efforts to make public transport more affordable and increase connections between rural and urban areas, hence decreasing demand in overcrowded housing areas in certain regions by making out-of-centre housing more attractive and reducing both rental and commuting cost of living for young people concerned.

* The reduction of taxes and introduction of tax credits for construction of housing rented under the local average rent and encouragement of

competition in the housing, transportation, and energy sectors to provide immediate assistance to all households.

* lifting regulations for housing construction in order to boost new development of affordable housing.

* Banning rental caps as a short-term solution to avoid long-term negative effects such as reduced housing supply, poorer living conditions, displacement of low-income tenants, and market distortions.

* Abolishing the real estate transfer tax in the long run.

* Providing tax allowances for first-time owner-occupied properties. These incentives can be adjusted based on property price development.

* Restricting objection rights to zoning changes and encouraging mixed-purpose and high-density zoning. Tied with general deregulation of housing and development laws to ease regulatory pressure. Tied with general deregulation of housing and development laws to ease regulatory pressure.
