*Context and Background*

* Noting the enormous growth of ultra-fast fashion platforms such as Shein and Temu, that rely heavily on dropshipping and direct-to-consumer imports from outside the EU, thus bypassing the traditional regulatory controls applied to physical stores and EU-based retailers.

  * Recognising that such platforms flood the market with thousands of new items every day, contributing to overconsumption and severe environmental damage, while avoiding most of the accountability for the products they sell.  
    * Concerned by hazardous chemicals found in clothing and in other items, sometimes in levels far exceeding limits set by the EU’s REACH regulation.  
    * Acknowledging that the VAT exemption for parcels under €150 has facilitated direct-to-consumer imports that often bypass proper regulatory enforcement, leading to inadequate oversight of product safety, chemical compliance, and environmental standards.  
    * Highlighting the environmental damage caused by ultra-fast fashion, including unsustainable production methods, massive amounts of textile waste, and the use of harmful chemicals such as PFAS (so-called "forever chemicals").  
    * Recognising the recent EU consumer protection action against Temu (November 2024), where the Consumer Protection Cooperation (CPC) Network of national authorities, coordinated by the European Commission, notified Temu of multiple breaches of EU consumer law which included fake discounts, misleading information, fake reviews, and refund policies, requiring Temu to bring its practices into compliance.  
    * Noting concerns which have been raised around the possible leaking of credit card information and payment details via Temu and the potential risks this has for the data privacy and information security of EU consumers.

* Recognising the European Commission’s proposal of 5 February 2025 to close loopholes associated with low-value imports, including the removal of the VAT exemption for parcels under €150, which currently distorts competition and enables regulatory evasion.

*Believing that:*

1. International trade benefits consumers through ensuring better quality, lower prices and promoting efficiency in global supply chains.  
2. International trade should be based on fair competition, meaning that all access to the Internal Market should be subject to the same rules and regulations.  
3. Dropshipping platforms, such as Shein and Temu, should be responsible for fulfilling regulatory requirements regarding product safety, chemical compliance, and environmental impact, not shifting this responsibility to individual consumers.  
4. All products imported directly to consumers, regardless of parcel size, value, or shipping method, should comply with EU chemical safety laws under REACH and related environmental standards.

5. The EU should close the "small parcel loophole", ensuring that all imports are subject to the same safety, transparency, and environmental requirements, whether they arrive via traditional retail supply chains or direct e-commerce platforms.

6. Platforms that facilitate mass imports of ultra-fast fashion must provide full transparency about product origins, materials, and chemical content, enabling consumers to make informed purchasing decisions.  
7. The EU must promote a shift away from the overconsumption model encouraged by ultra-fast fashion platforms, towards a sustainable consumption, including circular economy principles and extended producer responsibility (EPR).  
8. European trade and customs policies must ensure fair competition between EU producers and external platforms, preventing environmental and consumer protection from being undermined by imports that do not meet the same standards.

9. The EU should promote innovation in sustainable textiles, encouraging circular design, high-quality durable products, and platforms that prioritise repair, reuse, and recycling.

*LYMEC calls for:*

* Binding EU legislation holding e-commerce platforms liable for ensuring that all products sold to EU consumers comply with chemical safety, environmental, and consumer protection laws.

* A revision of the E-commerce Directive, ensuring platforms like Shein and Temu are treated as importers, making them fully responsible for product compliance.  
* The digitisation of all remaining customs on imports into the EU in the framework of the ongoing Customs Reform to ensure quick but controlled market entry

* EU-wide consumer awareness campaigns highlighting the risks of ultra-fast fashion, including health hazards from toxic chemicals, environmental concerns, and exploitation in the supply chain.

* Cutting red tape and incentivising private investment as support for sustainable alternatives, including second-hand markets, repair and reuse initiatives, and EU-based sustainable fashion startups.

* Fair competition rules ensuring that EU retailers and producers are not disadvantaged by platforms exploiting lower environmental and safety standards outside the EU.

* The EU to enter into more Free Trade Agreements (FTA) with countries all around the world, thus ensuring fair access to the Internal Market.
