Submitted by: Silvia Fernández (IMS), Joventut Nacionalista de Catalunya (JNC), LHG

*Considering that:*

* Advancements in technology and digital sectors are critical for economic growth, competitiveness, and innovation.

* The EU’s geopolitical influence and independence is determined, among other things, by its economic competitiveness and capacity for technological innovation.

* The EU has a significant potential to excel in innovation and entrepreneurship. However, the absence of leading companies and a strong cluster environment presents challenges in retaining top talent.  
* Effective policies, initiatives and a unified market are essential to foster a thriving tech ecosystem within the EU to compete globally.  
* Enhancing market relevance and competitiveness requires a collaborative effort among member states, industry stakeholders, and educational institutions.  
* European education systems primarily prepare individuals for traditional employment instead of promoting entrepreneurial skills and innovation.  
* Supporting startups, scale-ups, and established tech companies is vital for sustaining innovation and technological advancement.  
* Existing European tech industries are currently fragmented amongst member states due to the lack of centralised regulation.  
* Excessive regulations and varying administrative requirements across EU member states create significant hurdles for entrepreneurs, making it harder to start and scale businesses in Europe.  
* Young entrepreneurs face significant challenges, including limited access to financing, insufficient industry connections, and a lack of targeted tax incentives or support programs.

*Recognising that:*

* The USA and China have established themselves as the current dominant players in the technology and digital sectors, attracting top talent and investment.

* Europe has historically been a cradle of world-leading innovation, underscoring its potential to return to the forefront of global research and development.

* The EU has already implemented initiatives such as Horizon Europe, but further efforts are needed to maintain and strengthen Europe’s position as a global innovation leader.

* The EU must create an European environment conducive to innovation, entrepreneurship, and collaboration with a consistent regulation across the EU to compete on a global scale.  
* Providing access to funding, resources, and infrastructure is crucial for nurturing startups and fostering growth in the tech industry.  
* Talent retention requires not only competitive salaries but also opportunities for professional development, work-life balance, and an inclusive work environment.

* Collaboration among industry, academia, and government is crucial for addressing skill gaps related to future jobs and for ensuring a consistent pipeline of qualified professionals and entrepreneurial education for those who will drive innovation and business creation.

* Access to funding remains a major challenge for young entrepreneurs, with limited venture capital and microfinance opportunities available compared to other global markets.

*Believing that:*

* A comprehensive collective strategy is necessary to retain talent, attract investment, and drive innovation in the EU's technology and digital sectors.

* Providing a supportive ecosystem for startups and tech companies will spur growth, create jobs, and enhance the EU's competitiveness.

* Fostering a culture of continuous learning and development is key to keeping the workforce agile and adaptable to technological advancements.

* Strengthening cooperation between universities, businesses, and policymakers can bridge the gap between research and market application.  
* Europe must create a pro-innovation regulatory environment that reduces barriers to business creation, enabling young entrepreneurs, especially in AI, blockchain, and fintech, to thrive and scale their startups.  
* A unified digital market with harmonized regulations on digital services, AI, and financial transactions is vital for Europe’s competitiveness.  
* The “European Startup Passport” should simplify the registration process across the EU, reducing administrative barriers and making it easier for startups to scale and compete internationally.  
* A European financial support plan should provide easier access to venture capital, microloans, and tax incentives to help young entrepreneurs overcome financial challenges, grow their business and advance to technological advancements.

*Therefore, LYMEC calls for:*

* The development and implementation of initiatives to retain talent, including competitive compensation packages, opportunities for professional development, and work-life balance policies.

* The creation of funding programs, grants, and incentives (e.g., tax reductions) to support start-ups, scale-ups, and tech companies, ensuring their access to capital and resources for growth.

* The establishment of innovation hubs and tech clusters throughout the EU to enhance collaboration and networking among technology professionals and entrepreneurs.  
* Promoting partnerships between industry and academia to address skill and research application to market gaps by developing relevant curricula in education systems

* Enhancing the digital infrastructure, including high-speed internet access, data centres, and cybersecurity measures, to support the tech industry.

* Encouraging diversity and inclusion initiatives to create a welcoming and supportive environment for all tech professionals, regardless of gender, ethnicity, or background.

* The EU to take steps to unify the tech industry market to provide domestic initiatives and startup companies a larger market.

* The creation of a 'European Startup Passport': A single digital platform for startup registration, compliance, and reporting across all EU member states, ensuring automatic recognition of business licenses and a fast-track process for highly innovative startups.

* Harmonizing regulations on digital services and AI, a Europe-wide digital identity framework for businesses, a pan-European open data strategy, and tax harmonization to simplify invoicing and cross-border payments.  
* A ‘NextGen Entrepreneurs Fund’ providing grants and microcredits, an expanded European Business Angels Network, a ‘Start & Scale’ performance-based financial support program, and tax reductions for young-founded startups in their first three years.  
* A Europe-wide incubator network, a 'Startup Erasmus' exchange program, the integration of entrepreneurship education in schools and universities, a European mentorship program, and stronger collaboration between universities and businesses to develop innovation hubs.

* A Public-Private European Investment Fund, a European Secondary Market for Startups to enhance liquidity, expanded tax incentives for venture capital investors, and simplified investment regulations to attract private and international funding.

* To reduce bureaucratic hurdles and faster approval processes for AI-driven and high-tech companies, ensuring a regulatory framework that fosters innovation while maintaining ethical and safety standards.
