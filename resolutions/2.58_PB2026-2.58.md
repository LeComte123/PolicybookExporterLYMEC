Cosignatories: LHG (Germany), RU (Denmark), LUF (Sweden), CUF (Sweden), Centerstudenter (Sweden), Adam Volf (IMS), Silvia Fernandez (IMS), Norges Unge Venstre (Norway), Gibanje Svoboda Mladi (Slovenia), Nowoczesna Youth (Poland), Jungfreisinnige Schweiz (Switzerland), Young Green Liberals (Switzerland), Youth Forum of Naša Stranka (Bosnia and Herzegovina), Young Progressive Slovakia (Slovakia), JUNOS (Austria), Joventut Nacionalista de Catalunya (JNC)

Considering that:

* being a part of the Schengen Area means having no internal border control, and therefore the member states do not carry out checks at their internal borders, except in cases of specific threats, and having harmonised controls carried out at the external borders of the Schengen Area;

  * there are currently 29 members in the Schengen Area, which include 25 of 27 EU Member States as well as all members of the European Free Trade Association;

    * since 2015 there has been a significant and continued increase in the number and duration of border control reinstatements;

    * Schengen members are bound by the Schengen Borders Code which is the set of rules governing the Schengen area;

* there are approximately 3.5 million people that cross internal borders daily for work, study, or to visit families and friends and an estimated 1.25 billion journeys within the sector every year;

  * the Schengen Borders Code sets out clear requirements for reinstating border controls, including the justification, duration, and procedural steps;

    * the Schengen Borders Code clearly states that the reintroduction of internal border controls should only take place as a last-resort option, and only when internal security or public policy are at serious risk and the reintroduction is an effective remedy to that risk;

    * the Schengen Borders Code is a part of EU law, but also binds the non EU member states in the Schengen Area;

    * the European Commission has, as guardian of the treaties, as one of its core duties to ensure the correct and timely compliance with and application of EU law;

    * the EU and its member states have established concrete measures to safeguard Europe’s security and reinforce the EU’s external borders, through the creation of Frontex and the Schengen Information System;

    * the ‘failure to act’-procedure, to force an EU institution to fulfil its duties under the treaties, cannot be brought against the European Commission for failing to start an infringement procedure by non-privileged parties;

    * mutual trust and sincere cooperation are foundational principles of the European Union;

    * reinstatements of border controls are a form of European disintegration, no matter the scale or size.

  Believing that:

    * free movement of people within Europe is a core pillar of European integration, essential to our rights as Europeans as well as our economic prosperity and the Schengen Zone its crowning achievement;

    * the condition for the absence of internal border controls are effective and reliable controls of the external borders of the EU;

    * a significant number of border control reinstatements in the Schengen Zone since 2015 have been pure political posturing, without addressing any real or significant threats, or not doing so effectively;

    * a large number of border control reinstatements do not meet the legal requirements set out in the Schengen Borders Code, be that in motivation, procedure or duration;

    * European disintegration for political posturing is directly opposed to the core principles of LYMEC, and as a tactic is a slippery slope that should never be attempted.

Calls for:

* Member States that have reinstated border controls to cease those controls as soon as possible;

  * the European Commission to execute its role as guardian of the treaties by rigorously enforcing the Schengen Borders Code;

    * Member States to start infringement procedures against violating Member States if the European Commission continues to fail to start these procedures, and for ALDE member parties to call for such action within their Member States;

    * all privileged parties, and specifically the European Parliament to begin a ‘failure to act’-procedure against the European Commission if it continues to fail to enforce the Schengen Borders Code, and for the RENEW Europe group in the European Parliament to call for such action;

    * Member States that have understandable concerns about border safety and lack of control over immigration flows to address those concerns through changing the policy of the EU’s external borders and immigration as well as increasing cooperation among authorities dealing with organised crime, instead of purely focussing on reinstating internal borders.

    * All entries into the Schengen area to be recorded and stored in a centralised, EU-wide system at the borders in order to prevent people from entering the EU under false identities. To this end, we welcome the planned introduction of the EU Entry/Exit System (EES) in the Schengen area.

    * To ensure further protection of the Schengen area's external borders, Frontex is to be expanded into an operational border police force with executive powers, with enhanced oversight by the European Parliament to ensure all human rights.

    * We want to extend the possibility of association with the European Union. To this end, we want to open the Schengen area to countries that are not members of the European Free Trade Association (EFTA).

    * The full accession of Bulgaria and Romania into the Schengen area.
