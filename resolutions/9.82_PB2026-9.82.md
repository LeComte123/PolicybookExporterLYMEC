**Passed Autumn 2025**

Youth, Jungfreisinnige Schweiz Youth, JUNOS  
Considering that:

* Development cooperation is an important part of the European CFSP.

  Providing

support to developing countries in key areas has been a high priority to date,

and it is important that this remains the case.

* Development cooperation has recently often been portrayed negatively in the

  media.

* Time and again, we get the impression that the financial resources provided under the umbrella of development cooperation to support states with weak  
  infrastructure and supply situations are not being used wisely.

* Well-organized and realistic development cooperation can provide significant

  support to structurally weak regions and countries that are often ravaged by

multiple crises, such as local conflicts or climate change.

* With the growing strategic considerations of many developing and emerging countries between turning to the West or to authoritarian states such as Russia

and China, this presents a key opportunity for economic partnerships.

* As part of the Belt and Road Initiative, China is gaining influence worldwide by providing funds for infrastructure projects such as bridges, ports, railways, and dams.  
* The goal of China is to create political dependence through diverse interdependencies. Approximately 150 countries worldwide have joined the Belt

and Road Initiative, including Austria, Italy, Portugal, and most countries on the African continent.

* Western development cooperation has failed to achieve clear deliverables in several decades. This has resulted in countless missed opportunities to work with partners such as in North Africa to increase prosperity in all participating countries and strengthen democratic institutions.  
* Industrialized countries bear a significant share of the responsibility for the tangible effects of climate change in Africa.  
* The North African countries are primarily transit countries for refugees from sub-Sahara Africa.

* The spread of jihadist movements across parts of Africa reflects deep governance

challenges, instability, and socioeconomic disparities that fuel radicalization and threaten both regional and European security.

Believing that:

* Development cooperation must focus primarily on the transfer of know-how and

innovation, rather than on one-off financial contributions that do not offer structural and therefore long-term solutions.

* Development cooperation should be brought more into the public eye, but it should also become more transparent in its own right, so that populist narratives and misinformation seeking to delegitimize ODA in general have less  
  fertile ground to grow.

* The EU's development cooperation must be aligned with the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

* It is crucial that the feasibility of these goals for developing countries is carefully examined at the multilateral level. In addition to environmental considerations, social and economic aspects must also be given

appropriate

consideration and carefully weighed up.

* Security cooperation is a fundamental pillar for Africa’s sustainable development, as stability and peace are prerequisites for economic growth,

investment, freedom, democracy, and effective governance.

Emphasizing that:

* Projects must generally be planned thoroughly and for the long term, implemented

  with as little bureaucracy as possible, and their impact adequately monitored.

  It must also be clearly stated and factored into the budget that development aid

  is intended to alleviate the causes of migration, thereby reducing the number of

people seeking refuge in Europe.

* In order to improve the image of development cooperation in society in the short  
  and long term, no money should be invested in projects that give the impression  
  of squandering financial resources.  
* Of the five North African countries—Morocco, Algeria, Libya, Tunisia, and Egypt—none are rated as stable by the Fragile State Index. Should these countries collapse, this would mean great suffering for the local population, a  
  loss of prosperity, the loss of a trading partner, and streams of refugees heading for Europe. The most important goal of development cooperation must  
  therefore be to stabilize countries and prevent them from failing. Efforts that go beyond stabilization must serve to promote democracy in partner countries.  
* The maintenance of inhumane detention camps, through which the EU shifts its  
  responsibility to areas not covered by its Convention on Human Rights, must be  
  stopped.

LYMEC calls for:

* Advocating for the targeted promotion of civil society organizations, local initiatives, and trustworthy partners in autocratically governed states, provided that this can be done transparently and in compliance with human rights  
  standards. Direct government payments should be ruled out in these cases.

* A clear distinction between non-repayable development aid in the form of financial assistance and loans that are to be repaid. A distinction should be made between the following two categories:

Category A: Know-how or expenditure only for essential structural improvements, such

as in the areas of health, food, and drinking water infrastructure, as well as for corresponding disaster relief efforts. The repayment of expenses is determined by contracts on a case-by-case basis. Climate protection projects may also fall under Category A in cases of particular urgency.

Category B: Financial support for non-essential structural improvements. This should include loans that are generally repayable with interest.

* Advocating for the close monitoring of projects in countries where corruption, authoritarian rule, or massive human rights violations exist. Projects with a proven impact on civil society—especially for women, minorities, and LGBTQ

  individuals—should remain possible, even if government conditions are difficult.

Projects involving government actors must be suspended if cooperation in compliance with human rights standards is not possible.

* The deepening of the CFSP in the European Union by pooling national development  
  cooperation measures at EU level in the long term, thereby saving costs by eliminating duplicate structures.  
* A serious European connectivity strategy on the African continent.  
* Focusing European development cooperation with African countries and the African  
  Union on sustainable job creation and knowledge transfer for the African population, as well as infrastructure development and new digital and climate  
  projects.  
* Increased cooperation between Western and African countries to remove barriers  
  to market entry in order to facilitate private investment in African countries. To this end, we want to expand and supplement existing free trade agreements  
  with the aim of establishing multilateral free trade agreements that enable the  
  free movement of goods and labor.

* Western countries to work together with their African partners to reduce bureaucracy, simplify visa procedures, make it easier to raise capital, and set

  up targeted support and advisory programs for companies wishing to invest in

  Africa.

* Using our relationships and resources to promote effective local solutions, since the ability of individual actors to take sufficient environmental and climate action is at times limited.

* Supporting African delegations in climate negotiations on a partnership basis.

* Using targeted support measures to promote the rapid expansion of solar power  
  plants in the Sahara in all North African countries. The goal should be to produce climate-neutral hydrogen locally that can also be used to power European  
  industry.

* Supporting our partners in creating humane refugee accommodation where asylum

applications to the European Union can be submitted directly.

* Concluding migration agreements between the European Union and North African

  countries, to stop human trafficking and illegal migration directly on the coasts of North Africa.

* Strengthening cooperation with African partners in intelligence sharing, training, and development support to more effectively combat jihadism and

address the root causes of violent islamic extremism.

* European development cooperation should place a particular focus on partner  
  countries that maintain effective cooperation on migration management and

readmission of rejected ayslum seekers.
