(Passed Spring 2025\)

Co-Signers: JNC, For Youth\! (Latvia), JUNOS, Jongerenorganisatie Vrijheid en Democratie, Jongliberalen, Venstres Ungdom, Silvia Fernandez (IMS), Radikal Ungdom (RU), Junge Liberale (JuLis), USR Tineret, Centerstudenter (CS), LHG, Norges Unge Venstre (NUV)

*Noting that:*

* the European Union is an actor that largely relies on soft power and international law on the global stage;

* a trade war is looming due to the unproved policy decisions of the Trump administration in the US;

* a policy of imposing incredibly high tariffs on all trade has historically clearly been economically detrimental to all parties;

* the Appellate Body of the World Trade Organization has since 2019 been unable to properly exercise its role as a protector of trade agreements, since the US has been blocking new appointments to it, resulting in it being unable to enforce WTO rules and punish violators of WTO rules,  
* where the EU has exclusive competence, meaning that only the EU can legislate on the issue and not the Member States, the EU alone has the power to negotiate and conclude an international agreement on the matter;

* where an international agreement also covers shared competences, where the Member States has the competence until there is EU legislation on the matter, the agreement becomes a so called ‘mixed agreement’ requiring not only the EU to conclude the agreement, but also each Member State to sign and ratify the treaty;

* this mixed trade agreement structure is currently required for agreements that cover elements such as intellectual property rights, social policy and consumer protection, Foreign Direct Investment beyond liberalization and other elements common in comprehensive trade agreements;

* there is a large amount of mixed agreements, that would significantly expand the opportunities for free trade for the EU, finalised or even signed by the EU that have not yet entered into force in large part because difficulty of getting it ratified by every single Member State;  
* the requirement of unanimity in the Council on certain trade agreements further complicates the matter of EU international agreements.

*Believing that:*

* more and freer trade is beneficial for the welfare of all countries involved, and can, when coupled with the right political and diplomatic cooperation, significantly strengthen the ties between free and democratic nations, as the EU itself has shown;

* in a world that seems to be turning back to trade wars and mercantilism, the EU, as a champion of free trade, should be seeking to turning the tide;  
* More access to non-US markets and giving more access to non-US markets can not only fill any potential gaps in loss of opportunity created by reduced free access between the EU and US markets, but can also create more economic opportunities;

* elements such as intellectual property rights, social policy and consumer protection, and Foreign Direct Investment beyond liberalization can be essential and desirable elements of comprehensive trade agreements;

* the EU makes itself a less attractive international partner with its current confusing system of mixed trade agreements, as well as the requirement of unanimity in the Council, since this might mean that even after all parties have spent decades concluding the agreement it can still become wasted effort;

*Calls for:*

* all EU Member States to rapidly ratify the existing trade agreements already signed by the European Union, particularly CETA.

* a rapid signing of the EU free trade agreements with Mercosur and Mexico, followed by a rapid ratification by all Member States should they be classified as mixed agreements;  
* the EU to rapidly pursue more and freer trade agreements, particularly with free and democratic countries or countries on a clear path to becoming freer and more democratic, be those agreements bilateral or multilateral;  
* the EU to intensify its cooperation in these matters with free and democratic partners, like Canada and Mexico, that have been unprovokedly and unfairly targeted by immense tariffs by the US;  
* the EU, its Member States and European states to work on reforming the WTO so that it can effectively function as a protector of trade, as well as no longer being able to be paralysed by the actions or inactions by one state or a small group of states;  
* the Treaties of the European Union to be revised so that international agreements can cover both exclusive as well as shared competences and still fall within the full competence of the EU, and thus not requiring the mixed agreement process;

* the Treaties of the European Union to be revised so that all international agreements concluded by the EU require Qualified Majority Voting when being voted on by the Council, in line with our position all votes taken by the European Council currently taken by unanimity should be taken by, at the very most, Qualified Majority Voting instead.
