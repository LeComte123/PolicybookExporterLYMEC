**Passed Autumn 2025**

Co-signed by: Unge Venstre (Norway), JNC (Catalonia), Momentum TizenX (Hungary),  
Silvia Fernandez (IMS), Young Liberals (UK), Radikal Ungdom (Denmark),

Jongliberalen

(Belgium), Liberala ungdomsförbundet (Sweden), Centerpartiets Ungdomsförbund (Sweden), JULIS (Germany)

Considering that:

* across Europe, transgender people are facing an uprise in discrimination, legal

  uncertainty, and political hostility;

* recent developments, such as the UK Supreme Court’s ruling limiting the definition of “woman” under equality law, and a surge in transphobic rhetoric  
  among politicians in several EU Member States, highlight an alarming rollback in  
  trans rights;

* Far-right parties and ultra-conservative groups have publicly used terms such as

  ‘transgender brainwashing’ and ‘queer experiments’ to stigmatise transgender and

  gender-diverse individuals

* Single issue ‘LGB’ groups which focus exclusively on advocating against Transgender rights under the guise of ‘protecting LGB’ rights are spreading across Europe with the aim of enacting explicitly anti-transgender laws

* these rhetorical attacks are part of a broad, internationally coordinated conservative agenda using trans people as scapegoats;

* these rhetorical and legal setbacks result in concrete threats against trans people.

Recognising that:

* equality, dignity, and respect are founding liberal values which must apply to all individuals regardless of gender identity or expression;  
* recent years have seen a considerable uptick in discrimination, hostility, and legal uncertainty for transgender people across Europe, including:

  * A rise in transphobic rhetoric among politicians in many EU Member States,

    including increased hate speech reported in countries such as Spain,

    Sweden, Croatia, Ireland, Slovakia, Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands, and

    Portugal.

  * European Fundamental Rights Agency (FRA) surveys showing that although the

    social acceptance of LGBTIQ+ people has increased overall, the incidence of

    hate-motivated harassment, physical and sexual attacks, particularly

    against trans, non-binary, and intersex people, is also increasing in many

    Member States.

  * An overall regression in the protection of trans people’s fundamental

    rights across Europe, as documented by the NGO TGEU in its 2025 Trans

    Rights Index.

* many European countries still lack adequate legal frameworks or have burdensome

  medical and diagnostic requirements for legal gender recognition;

* Gender affirming healthcare such as hormone replacement therapy has been proven

  repeatedly to improve the quality of life and mental health of transgender people

* public services, including schools and health services amongst other institutions, often fail to meet the needs of transgender people, leading to discrimination, exclusion and marginalisation.

Reaffirms that:

* liberal movements must continue to champion the rights of transgender people as

  an integral part of defending freedom, equality, and human dignity across Europe;

* we believe that liberals must respond decisively to defend freedom, equality, and human dignity for all.

Calls for:

* The European Commission to encourage and support Member States in the development and implementation of concrete national actions, guided by the  
  objectives set out in the European Commission’s LGBTIQ+ equality strategy 2026 \-  
  2030, with a specific focus on transgender inclusion, and to regularly monitor

  progress in eliminating systemic bias and discrimination at the national level.

* Member States to develop national action plans for trans inclusion with clear timelines, accountability mechanisms, and measurable outcomes;

* the reform and modernisation of gender recognition laws across Europe to establish simple, affordable, and transparent administrative procedures based on  
  statutory declaration;

* the abolition of the obligation to provide medical or psychiatric expertise for the legal gender recognition procedure;

* European governments to work to improve the availability of gender affirming health care, including but not limited to hormone replacement therapy for transgender, gender non-conforming, and gender dysphoric patients  
* health services excluding the pathologisation of trans identities;

* European governments and institutions to promote inclusive education and to implement comprehensive anti-bullying policies;

* public services that are designed and reviewed to meet the needs of transgender

  and gender-diverse people;

* the European Commission and Member States to condemn and counter the spread of

  transphobic disinformation and hate speech, especially through anti-discrimination legal norms;

* governments to support independent research and civil society initiatives documenting discrimination and violence against trans people;

* Member States to collect disaggregated data and establish indicators to monitor

progress on trans inclusion, including access to legal recognition, hate crime resolution, and inclusive education.
