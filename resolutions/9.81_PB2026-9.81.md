**Passed Autumn 2025**

Noting that:

* The Arctic, which has long been characterised by security policy stability with a historically low risk of escalation, has seen a greater power competition over

  the last years.

* Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022,, and its growing strategic interest in Arctic resources and routes have shifted it in many Western perceptions from partner to challenger.

* Instances of uncertainty in U.S. foreign policy (e.g. threats over Greenland) have strained trust in alliances and emphasised European dependence on U.S.  
  security guarantees.

* Several European NATO members, especially those in or near the Arctic, have increased concern over Russian military buildup and ambitions in the High North.

* In late September 2025, unauthorised suspected Russian drone activity was observed near multiple airports in Denmark and Norway, causing major disruptions

  and was a reminder of Russian presence in the north.

Emphasising that:

* The security environment in Europe, especially due to Russia’s behavior, demands

  stronger collective defence capabilities and clearer responsibilities among NATO

  members.

* In recent years, Russia has demonstrated an increasing interest in exploiting the many opportunities in the Arctic, such as oil, natural gas, and potential future shipping routes made accessible by climate change.  
* Russia’s ongoing construction of a new military base on Wrangel Island demonstrates its increasing militarisation and strategic interest in the Arctic, with potential implications for NATO operations, environmental protection, and

  the stability of the region.

* Existing frameworks such as the Nordic Defence Cooperation (NORDEFCO) already

  provide a strong foundation for enhancing regional coordination, interoperability, and preparedness in the Arctic region.

* Legal norms and international order (treaties, maritime law, freedom of navigation) are fundamental to stability in the Arctic and must be defended

  accordingly.

* European NATO members have the capacity and interest to take greater ownership  
  of defence in the High North, reducing over-reliance on U.S. presence, but this  
  requires planning, coordination, capability investment, and credible command

  structures.

* The current geopolitical situation in Europe necessitates more proactive European NATO members, ensuring the alliance's interests while preventing Russia’s protectionist developments in the region.

* Nearly all members of the Arctic Council, except Russia, are NATO members, and  
  the risk of escalation does not appear to outweigh the dangers of lacking a presence in the region.

Believing that:

* To protect Europe’s broad range of interests, the Arctic must remain a peaceful

and stable region based on international cooperation and respect for legal principles.

* While seeking collaboration, European countries in NATO must secure adequate

  defence capabilities and preparedness to ensure sovereignty and security in the

  northern areas.

* Arctic security must also be a top priority within the EU defence cooperation and NORDEFCO. Whereas the EU’s role in security policy has sparked debate in

  countries like Denmark, which lifted its defence opt-out in European cooperation, the debate has been largely absent in Norway, despite the fact that

  modern security also encompasses non-military dimensions such as cyber defence,

  energy resilience, disinformation, and supply chain security.

LYMEC calls for:

1. Uphold of International Legal Order

   * Maintain strict adherence to UNCLOS (United Nations Convention on the Law of the

   Sea) and other binding international treaties relevant to Arctic sovereignty, navigation, and maritime boundaries.

   * Oppose unilateral changes to recognised boundaries or restrictions of navigation

   routes without a legal basis.

   * Support adjudication / legal mechanisms for overlapping sovereignty claims.

   * Prevent Russia from violating international commitments regarding freedom of

   navigation through the Northwest Passage and the Northeast Passage.

2. Strengthened Arctic Defence Posture among European NATO Members

   * Increase investment in military capabilities suited for Arctic conditions: cold weather gear, ice-capable vessels (incl. icebreakers), long-range surveillance,

   naval / air assets able to operate in polar climates.

   * Ensure a more balanced burden-sharing within NATO to reduce reliance on the US

   for defence.

   * Establish a clearer, unified, regional command or coordination mechanism within

   NATO for the Arctic theatre (while respecting national sovereignty).

3. Balanced Burden-Sharing within NATO

* European NATO states are expected to increase their defence spending, particularly for an Arctic/Northern posture, not just in aggregate, but also for

  targeted capabilities.

  * Coordinated procurement and joint development of infrastructure, logistics, and

  systems for the High North to avoid duplication and reduce cost.

  * Transparent contribution reporting and mutual accountability.

4. Nordic & Arctic Regional Cooperation under NATO & EU Frameworks

   * Reinforce cooperation among European & Arctic states in joint exercises, intelligence sharing, infrastructure, in NATO, NORDEFCO and EU contexts.

   * Engage with local/regional authorities/communities (including indigenous populations) in planning and oversight where territory/sovereignty / local impact are involved.  
   * Strengthen the work carried out by NATO countries within the Arctic Council.

   * Enhance NORDEFCOcooperation in parallel with NATO collaboration.
