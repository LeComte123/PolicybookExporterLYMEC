(Passed Spring 2025\)

Submitted by: Junge Liberale (JuLis)

Co-signed by: Bundesverband Liberaler Hochschulgruppen (LHG), CUF, ERPY, JNC, Jonge Democraten, JOVD, Jungfreisinnige Schweiz, JUNOS, Lithuanian Liberal Youth,

LUF, Nowoczesna Youth, Radikale Ungdom, Venstres Ungdom, Young Liberals UK, Molodezhka

Considering that:

* The worsening human rights situation in Tibet, particularly the Tibetan Autonomous Region (TAR). Including reports of arbitrary arrests, unfair trials, inhumane detention conditions, and torture.  
* The absence of freedom of expression in Tibet, particularly in the TAR.

* The absence of freedom of religion in Tibet, particularly in the TAR.

* The absence of freedom of movement in Tibet, particularly in the TAR.

* The imperial language policy of the People's Republic of China, which increasingly sidelines the Tibetan language in public life within Tibet, particularly in the TAR.

* Reports of the planned constructions of water dams and reservoirs in populated areas of the Tibetan high plateau and the danger of ecological disasters.  
* The potential cutoff of water-flows in rivers originating in the Tibetan high plateau for downstream riparian countries in Southeast Asia and South Asia.  
* The restrictions on reports and expressions regarding Tibet on Chinese media and social media, including Chinese owned enterprises which operate abroad, such as TikTok.  
* The official discourse of the PRC which has replaced the term Tibet with the sinicized term “Xizang” in its official documents and statements concerning Tibet.

Believing that:

* In the future of a free and democratic Tibet.

* Freedom of speech, freedom of expression, freedom of religion and freedom of movement are fundamental human rights, which no human being shall be restricted from exercising.

* The Tibetan language, as other regional languages in the PRC, should be a language of instruction in schools.

* The Central Tibetan Administration (CTA), its democratically elected representatives – including the Sikyong and the members of the Tibetan Parliament-in-exile – is a torchbearer for Tibetan democracy.

* The CTA should see increasing global support for its cause to resolve the Tibet conflict in a peaceful manner, according to the right of self-determination.

* The CTA is the only authority, as the democratic representation of Tibetans around the world, which shall negotiate the issue of Tibet with the government of the PRC.

* The current “Umaylam” (Middle-Way Approach) is a suitable option to restore peace and dignity to Tibetans living in Tibet, especially those living in the TAR, as long as it is the Tibetans.  
* The EU should voice their support for the cause of the CTA, vis-à-vis the government of the PRC.

Emphasizing that:

* Tibet – including the TAR and parts of Qinghai, Sichuan and Yunnan – is an independent country under occupation of the PRC since 1950\.

* Historical Tibet – before 1950 – has been an independent country which exercised diplomatic relations with other countries.

* The illegal annexation of Tibet by the forces of the People's Liberation Army (PLA) of the PRC was a grave violation of international law, for which the PRC has never had to take responsibility.

* The institution of the Dalai Lama – including the current 14th Dalai Lama, Tenzin Gyaltso – is of invaluable importance for the Tibetan people.

LYMEC thus calls for:

* The EU and its member states to clearly condemn the human rights violations in the TAR and Tibetan regions of Qinghai, Sichuan and Yunnan. Political asylum must be granted to dissidents from Tibet.

* The EU and its member states to maintain a regular dialogue with the exiled representatives of the CTA, especially the Sikyong and the members of the Kashag and the Tibet Bureau in Brussels.

* The EU to acknowledge the Middle-Way Approach to seek a peaceful solution to the Tibet conflict.

* The EU to acknowledge the status of Tibet as an independent country before the illegal annexation of the PRC.

* The European Parliament to send a delegation of parliamentarians to the Tibetan Parliament-in-exile.

* The EU to only support the legitimacy of religious reincarnations of Tibetan religious institutions, such as the Dalai Lama or Panchen Lama, which have been approved by the legitimate democratic representation of the Tibetan people, which is the CTA.  
* The EU and its member states to advocate internationally for the continued public teaching of Tibetan in Tibetan schools in the PRC.  
* The EU and its member states to condemn any threats of public bans of the Tibetan language.  
* The EU and its member states to voice their concern, regarding the transnational effects in Southeast Asia and South Asia due to PRC-led construction projects that would affect the flow of water in rivers originating in the Tibetan high plateau.  
* The EU and its member states support the U.S. “Reciprocal access to Tibet Act” and to implement legislation following the U.S. “Reciprocal Access to Tibet Act”.

* Free access to the TAR and adjacent Tibetan settlements, including the freedom of movement for Tibetans.

* All internationally recognized NGOs be granted access to Tibet and not be hindered in their work. NGOs, particularly those not recognized under Chinese national law but recognized internationally, should be included.

* Representatives of the press must be granted unlimited access to Tibet. Government censorship, influence, or intimidating surveillance of the press must be stopped.

* Diplomatic personnel from the European Union and its member states must be granted unlimited access to Tibet.

* Any official responsible for denying access should be denied entry to the European Union under the principle of diplomatic reciprocity. This applies only to employees and officials of the Chinese authorities. Employees of the Chinese press, Chinese NGOs, or Chinese private individuals should not be affected by this regulation.

* The High Representative and either the European External Action Service or the European Union Institute for Security Studies should produce an annual report on access possibilities for the above-mentioned groups to Tibet, naming those responsible for denying entry.
