Adopted at the Spring Congress 2023 in Budapest, Hungary on 6 May 2023\.

**Noting that:**

* Approximately 4,1 million patients are estimated to acquire a healthcare associated infection (HAI) in the EU each year of which around 37 000 die as a direct consequence of the infection.

  * About 50-100% of the healthcare associated infections can be prevented by basic hygiene routines.

    * Healthcare associated infections are the most common healthcare injuries in the EU.

    * 15% of the healthcare budgets in OECD countries are used to treat healthcare injuries. Money that could be used to treat more patients and investing in research.

    * The lengthened time of care for patients acquiring HAIs prevents others from their necessary care and treatments.

  The continuous increase of health-care associated infections caused by antimicrobial-resistant microorganisms that are difficult to treat, contributes to the morbidity and mortality of HAIs as well as higher healthcare costs.

    * The number of antimicrobial-resistant microorganisms in Europe is increasing rapidly and is the cause of death for 33 000 people yearly.

    * Effective HAI prevention and control in healthcare organisations relies on specialised healthcare specialities in charge of elaborating, implementing, and monitoring preventive measures.

**Considering that:**

* Not all EU/EEA Member States have national recommendations for the management of infection control and hospital hygiene. In even fewer member states are they defined by law.

  * 63% of doctors and 77% of nurses in EU/EEA Member states report having a professional profile for infection control and hospital hygiene doctors and nurses. However, approximately half of the Member States do not have a curriculum or programme for training doctors and nurses as infection control and hospital hygiene professionals.

    * Basic training in infection control and hospital hygiene is only mandatory in 58% of medical schools and 66% of nursing schools in the EU.  
    * A high percentage of EU/EEA Member States seek EU-cooperation in managing a surveillance system and investigating healthcare-associated infections.

    * The lack of funding for infection control and hospital hygiene training programmes are perceived as one of the main constraints to battling HAIs.  
    * The definition of good hygiene is very vague. It is of great value to have a standardised hygiene protocol for EU/EEA Member States to prevent local discrepancy.

    * A higher percentage of antimicrobial-resistant microorganisms are present in hospitals compared to other parts of society.

**Believing that:**

No patient nor healthcare professional should risk mortality or morbidity caused by an acquired healthcare associated infection that could have been prevented.

* One of the most effective ways to tackle the growing problem of antimicrobial-resistance is to stop the spread of microorganisms in hospitals.

**Calls for:**

* The undertaking of regulatory documents enforcing basic hygiene routines and infection control in EU healthcare to prevent healthcare associated infections and thus minimising hospital acquired injuries and the spread of antimicrobial resistance.  
  * All EU/EEA Member States to offer a curriculum or programme for training doctors and nurses as infection control and hospital hygiene professionals.  
    * All medical- and nursing schools in the EU to have basic hygiene routines and infection control as a mandatory part of the education.  
    * EU-cooperation in designing and managing a European surveillance system to evaluate infection control and hygiene routines to prevent healthcare associated infections.

  29.  **Cutting the Clutter to Bust Bureaucracy: Removing Bureaucratic Hurdles from ERASMUS+**

**Noting that:**

* The ERASMUS+ exchange program is one of the European Union's most successful and acclaimed initiatives, having positively impacted the lives of millions of students, enhancing cross-cultural understanding and academic growth.

* Despite its general success, the European Council is proposing to cut the budget for ERASMUS+ by 300 million euros in 2025\.

* Furthermore, a considerable number of students and educational institutions have voiced concerns about the burdensome bureaucratic processes associated with participating in the program.  
* The high bureaucratic burdens of the current ERASMUS+ program have been acknowledged by the European Parliament’s Committee on Culture and Education, recognizing it as being “excessively bureaucratic” in its report A9-0413/2023.  
* High bureaucratic burdens of entry might prevent students from less privileged backgrounds or with less access to qualified assistance from participating in the ERASMUS+ program. In addition, the high bureaucratic burdens also pose a challenge to better-off students, who have to devote a significant amount of time and effort to navigate the arduous application process.  
* A streamlined and bureaucratically efficient procedure not only encourages wider participation of students but also reduces administrative costs, ensuring that resources can be used more productively.

**Believing that:**

* The ERASMUS+ exchange program's true potential lies not only in facilitating academic exchanges but also in forging a connected, educated, and united Europe.  
* Reducing bureaucratic burdens will make the program more accessible to students from all backgrounds, ensuring that everyone has an equal opportunity to benefit from it.  
* Enabling more students to participate in the ERASMUS+ programme will not only positively impact their personal lives, but also boost academic exchange throughout Europe, benefit the economies of the host countries, and strengthen European integration.  
* A leaner and more intuitive application process will enable educational institutions to spend their resources more efficiently and to focus more on academic collaboration rather than paperwork.  
* A reduction of bureaucratic burdens would give effect to liberal values such as the belief in the equality of opportunity for everyone, the commitment to promoting efficiency, the facilitation of access to education, and fostering international cooperation.

**Notwithstanding that:**

* International academic exchange programs involving travels to foreign countries, the transaction of financial funds, and the seamless recognition foreign-earned study credits inevitably require a certain degree of bureaucratic processes.  
* The reduction of bureaucratic burdens may under no circumstances come at the expense of negatively affecting the quality of education or the safety and wellbeing of the students.

**LYMEC calls for:**

* A comprehensive review of the current bureaucratic processes associated with the ERASMUS+ exchange program, identifying existing bottlenecks and locating areas for improvement.  
* The elimination of superfluous bureaucratic processes, such as the redundant collection of personal data, with the aim of creating a more efficient and streamlined process.  
* The European Commission and European Parliament to reject the proposed budget cut of ERASMUS+.  
* The elimination of superfluous bureaucratic processes, such as the redundant collection of personal data, with the aim of creating a more efficient and streamlined process.  
* Training programs for administrators at educational institutions to ensure a consistent understanding and execution of the streamlined processes, thereby enabling them to guide and assist students more effectively.  
* The introduction of a centralised ERASMUS+ online platform that simplifies the application process, tracks student progress, and offers real-time support to both students and educational institutions.  
* Enhanced coordination between sending and receiving institutions of higher education, so that agreements about mutual recognition of study credits can be transparently made before the beginning of the exchange.  
* Periodic feedback collection from both participating students and educational institutions to continually refine and improve the streamlining process.
