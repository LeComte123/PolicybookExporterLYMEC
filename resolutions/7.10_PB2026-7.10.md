Noting that:

* Antibiotics are used extensively and excessively in agriculture for disease prevention and growth promotion in livestock and poultry. Today, animals are often given treatment even if they are healthy, which is a waste and threatens the ability to combat diseases.  
* It contributes to the global threat of antibiotics resistance, which is an urgent and growing issue.

* Already, 35 000 people die annually in Europe due to this resistance according to the European Centre for Disease prevention and Control.

* The World Health Organization states that the antibiotic clinical pipeline is “insufficient to tackle the challenge of increasing emergence and spread of antimicrobial resistance”.  
* There is low financial incentive for pharmaceutical companies to participate in the development of new antibiotics due to the resistance concerns (if resistance increases rapidly the new drugs may become ineffective), high development costs and low profit margins compared to other drugs, as well as problematic regulations such as requiring proof that the new treatment is superior to the existing ones, which is difficult to achieve in the context of antibiotic-resistant infections.  
* The excessive prescription of antibiotics in human and veterinary medicine and the subsequent usage contributes to the increase of resistant bacteria.

Believing that:

* There are other, evidence-based ways for disease prevention, such as improved hygiene and vaccination which would reduce the reliance of antibiotics.  
* The rapidly growing threat of antibiotics resistance causes a need for regulation.

Therefore, LYMEC calls for:

* Strengthened regulatory frameworks and oversight mechanisms of the European

  Medicines Agency to monitor and control the use of antibiotics in agriculture,including prohibitions on the use of medically important antibiotics for growth promotion and prophylactic purposes.

* Encouraging investments in research and innovation to develop alternative strategies for disease prevention, such as novel vaccines, probiotics and prebiotics.  
* Boosting the research for antibiotics in Europe, for example through Transferable Exclusivity Vouchers for the development of so-called “priority antimicrobials”. The voucher shall give the company an additional year of regulatory data protection and it can choose to keep this voucher or transfer it to another product by selling it. This would especially give smaller companies a chance to compete in the highly competitive pharmaceutical market and expand the pool of developers.  
* Rational prescriptions of antibiotics in medicine, internal warning systems for overprescriptions and specialised training about infectious diseases for internal physicians and general practitioners.  
* Require agricultural import companies to comply with European regulations regarding the antibiotics, ensuring fair competition and preventing unfair practices.
