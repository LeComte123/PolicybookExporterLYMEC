**Passed Autumn 2025**

(UK), Jóvenes de Ciudadanos (Spain) Considering that:

* Europe is increasingly facing internal and external threats to its peace and security, including regional conflicts, potential terrorism, hybrid warfare, cyber threats, erosion of democratic structures, human rights violations, and economic instability;  
* Security is the foundation and precondition of economic prosperity, stability, human rights and democracy within and around the European Union;  
* To secure European interests and liberal values, the EU must complement soft power with defence-oriented capabilities, such as strong alliances and well-equipped and coordinated militaries that strengthen diplomacy and deterrence,  
  while prioritising conflict prevention, de-escalation, and international law;

* The Russian Federation’s unlawful invasion of Ukraine and its continuing hybrid war waged upon the EU and its allies and partners demonstrate the reality of

  these threats and the need for prompt, decisive action;

* The Russian Federation’s unlawful invasion of Ukraine and its continuing hybrid war waged upon the EU and its allies and partners demonstrate the reality of

  these threats and the need for prompt, decisive action;

* The Russian Federation’s unlawful invasion of Ukraine and its continuing hybrid war waged upon the EU and its allies demonstrate the reality of these threats

  and the need for prompt, decisive action;

* The Russian Federation could well start another major war on the European continent within five years;

Noting that:

* EU Member States maintain 27 largely parallel defence systems. This fragmentation creates duplication in planning, procurement and sustainment,

  leading to inefficient use of funds that closer cooperation and interoperability

  can reduce;

* CSDP already provides the EU’s security and defence framework, now guided by the

  2022 Strategic Compass with a concrete implementation timetable;

* Practical cooperation runs through PESCO for collaborative capability development, the European Defence Fund for joint R\&D/procurement, and the

  European Defence Agency to ensure coherence between these institutions and with

  NATO;

Recalling that:

* LYMEC already supported closer European military cooperation in 2019, and that

the security situation in Europe has dramatically worsened since; LYMEC calls for:

* European Member States to intensify the Common Security and Defence Policy  
  (CSDP) under the Strategic Compass and, as a practical step toward

greater

European defence integration, prioritise joint planning, standardisation and transparent defence procurement, all to be subject to robust parliamentary scrutiny;

* Member organisations to advocate within the ALDE group for deeper European  
  defence cooperation, centred on interoperable, deployable CSDP forces and shared  
  enablers, as well as initiate discussions on reforming and integrating European-

  level defence and security institutions with the long-term vision of a unified European defence force under clear civilian control and parliamentary oversight.

  The goals of such an institution should be limited in scope, to ensure that the sovereignty of all member states is safeguarded: securing European territory,

  protecting international peace and stability, as well as disaster relief and humanitarian aid, both inside and outside of the EU;

* All European states to maintain their defence spending at no less than 2% of GDP  
  in accordance with the 2006 agreement, while progressively aiming toward the 5%  
  of GDP target set under the NATO 2025 commitments;  
* EU member states that are currently not members of NATO (Austria, Cyprus, Malta) to join NATO.

* Europe to reach a defense readiness level by 2030 such as to be able to deter,

  and if necessary fight and win, against a peer threat such as Russia;

* Member states, European institutions, member organisations and ALDE to ensure

  public communication which demonstrate that coordinated defence efforts

  strengthen, rather than diminish, national sovereignty by improving interoperability, cross-border mobility and access to shared enablers that no single Member State can field alone, while respecting subsidiarity and national

  prerogatives;

* Reinforced counter-disinformation, cyber defence and hybrid-threat resilience

  under CSDP and existing EU frameworks: shared situational awareness, rapid-

  response support to Member States (including electoral-process protection and

  critical-infrastructure defence), common exercises, and clear safeguards for

media freedom, data protection and fundamental rights, implicating non-EU

European partners where sensible;

* The Republic of Malta to join PESCO, so that all Member States are part of the framework;

* Rapid scaling of Europe’s defence industrial base to build lasting production capacity that could reliably supply and sustain allied forces in crises and reduce dependency on non-European suppliers, while ensuring strong democratic

  oversight, clear limits on sales and use, and full respect for human rights and non-proliferation;

* Prioritisation of joint European procurement, production and fast-ramp industrial mechanisms, such as grants, loans, via the European Defence Fund, and  
  public-private partnerships to convert short-term procurement into sustainable  
  capacity. Procurement efforts must be joint, more rapid and iterative.

* The implementation of strong EU mechanisms to ensure rigorous export controls

  and strict end-use monitoring on all EU-made weapons, ammunition and military

  materiel;

* The implementation of strong mechanisms to ensure democratic control and safeguards over European defence and security structures, covering but not

  limited to budgeting, mandates, operational and strategic directions, guaranteed

  transparency, accountability, democratic legitimacy and full respect for International Humanitarian Law, International Human Rights Law and liberal values;

* European states and the EU to also focus on conflict prevention through development aid, diplomacy, police, justice, sanctions, cyber capabilities and

  defence so that crises don’t escalate into long-term violent conflicts.
