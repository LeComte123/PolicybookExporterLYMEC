**Passed Autumn 2025**

Submitted by: Junge liberale NEOS (Austria)

Co-signed by: Norges Unge Venstre (Norway), Joventut Nacionalista de Catalunya (Catalonia), ZeMolodizhka (Ukraine), Jongliberalen (Belgium), Jóvenes de Ciudadanos

(Spain), JOVD (Netherlands), Jungfreisinnige Schweiz (Switzerland) This resolution archives resolution 2.27 and 2.39

*As an organization of liberal youth parties, we recognize the importance of addressing the question of asylum policy with a clear and balanced approach that is*

*neither blindly idealistic nor driven by fear. While parties on the left often focus solely on our humanitarian responsibility, and those on the right only raise concerns regarding security and culture, we seek a rational, solution-oriented path that strengthens Europe. Our goal is not to close borders, but to develop an asylum policy*

*that promotes integration, safeguards public order, and upholds European values, all*

*the while providing protection to those fleeing persecution and war.*

Noting that:

* While migration and asylum are closely associated terms, a clear distinction is necessary to effectively discuss and create policy regarding these issues.

  While

  migration encompasses a broader definition of people moving to another country

  for reasons of education, work, or family, asylum is defined as those seeking protection on the basis of being persecuted due to their religion, political beliefs, ethnicity or sexual orientation, and those fleeing from war and violence.

* Integration of admitted asylum seekers has not been as successful as was hoped  
  at the onset of the refugee crisis in 2015\. Labor force participation of those having arrived from the MENA region, Somalia, or Afghanistan is low \[1\]\[2\]\[3\],

cultural assimilation has been slow, and criminality of these groups is at a significantly elevated level compared to nationals, or migrants from most other

regions \[4\]\[5\]\[6\]\[7\].

Believing that:

* We have a moral responsibility towards those fleeing war, famine, and persecution.

* While certain groups of asylum seekers may face challenges integrating into the

  labor market due to discrimination and lack of access to education, migrants

  contribute significantly to Europe's economy and social fabric in the long term.

* Providing asylum to those with a justified claim must be a shared responsibility among member states.  
* Cooperation between member states will yield fairer and more efficient results,  
  compared to certain member states having to shoulder a disproportionate burden

  due to their geographic location, undercutting one another to make the country

  unattractive to asylum seekers, or attempting to repatriate denied asylum claimants on their own.

* Europe will continue to attract many millions of migrants, due to its quality of life, job opportunities, welfare systems, and not least of all, the often desolate situation in origin countries. Of these millions, many will attempt to migrate on the grounds of asylum. Having efficient and well-functioning legislation is crucial to being able to help those deserving our protection, while quickly turning away those without a justified claim to asylum.  
* The conviction to help people in need is juxtaposed with the practical consideration that we do not have an unlimited capacity for accepting asylum  
  seekers within the EU and associated states. To help those most desperately in  
  need of protection and deliver effective assistance, we must keep the number of  
  people claiming asylum in the EU and associated states at a manageable level.

LYMEC \- European Liberal Youth calls for:

* Harmonizing asylum procedures across member and associated states to enable

  faster processing of asylum claims.

* all European asylum procedures to uphold the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights

  and the Refugee Convention, whereby efficiency goes hand in hand with humane

  reception and fair procedures. Recognised refugees should have access to language training, education, and work to support their integration and participation in European society.

* A fair distribution of all refugees between member and associated states and suspension of the option for member states to provide only financial or organizational support instead of accepting asylum seekers, as this would once

  again lead to an unsolidary distribution of those in need of protection.

* Registering all asylum claimants at the external border without fail to accurately document their entry date and travel history, facilitate their distribution among member and associated states, and enable the immediate

  rejection of claimants who are clearly ineligible for asylum.

* A residence obligation for the asylum seeker in the country that is responsible for processing the asylum claim.

* Intensify efforts to sign agreements on returning asylum seekers who have been

  denied asylum, or have been stripped of their asylum status with their destination countries or safe third countries, who signal readiness to accept these persons.

* If a country of origin persistently refuses to cooperate on the return of its citizens, the European Union should stop its development cooperation and review  
  trade relations with that country, applying conditionality through diplomatic  
  pressure and targeted incentives while maintaining projects that promote human

  rights, democratic institutions, and local economic opportunities.

* The revocation of protection status if the applicant travels to their home country, or another non-EU or non-associated country.  
* The suspension of the asylum claim if the applicant leaves the country processing the claim.  
* The expulsion of persons with a legally binding negative decision on their need  
  for protection within one week.

* Effective implementation of the denial or revocation of asylum protection in cases of serious crimes under Articles 2 and 3 of the ECHR.

* Stronger cooperation and joint action by Member and associated States in the

  fight against human smuggling, a criminal and morally repugnant enterprise

  behind unacceptable and well-documented cases of human suffering and death on

  European territory and beyond.

* Increased efforts to further professionalize Frontex, in order to better protect the external borders while showing the utmost respect for human life and health.

* An increased focus on providing protection to people in safe places of the region from which asylum seekers are fleeing, for reasons of being more accessible, lessening the reliance on human smuggling to gain protection, being

  significantly more cost-effective, and allowing for a quick return home once the

  reasons for claiming asylum subside.

* Strengthen and accelerate the EU’s efforts to conclude return agreements, obliged to leave, with destination countries or safe third countries that are willing to cooperate, enabling effective repatriations of individuals who have been denied or lost their asylum status. Such agreements should include incentives for partner countries, such as easier access to working visa for highly skilled or needed professionals, or increased development aid.

* The possibility of comprehensive language and vocational training programs made

  in partnership with the private sector for all those granted asylum to ensure successful integration into the workforce and full participation in European society, as part of a broader effort to equip refugees with the necessary skills

  should they resettle. These programs should prioritize long-term employability,

  access to education, and skills development to reduce dependency on social

  welfare and help avoid segregation in marginalized communities while maximising

  economic potential.

* Addressing the causes of asylum migration and not only the consequences that are

  visible in Europe.
