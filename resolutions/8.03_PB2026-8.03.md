**Passed Autumn 2025**

LYMEC notes that:

* High speed rail connectivity among EU major cities is still limited, with several key-cross border links still missing or incomplete.  
* Cross border coordination among member states remains a major challenge for the  
  development of the European High-speed Network: different national regulations,  
  technical standarts and continuing interoperability of security, communication  
  and signaling systems. This fragmentation also includes variations in track gauge, electrification systems, platform heights, servicing practices, and train  
  control technologies, which increase costs and delay cross-border operations.  
* Short-haul flights continue to be the dominant transportation between major European cities due to their lower comparative prices and better connectivity,  
  despite their significant environmental impact.

* The Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) and European Rail Traffic Management System (ERTMS) are important frameworks but face delays in implementation and funding.

* While the European Union Agency for Railways (ERA) is essential for coordination

  among Member States, further commitment on integration remains necessary.

LYMEC believes that:

* Unifying the European high-speed rail network would serve multiple policy objectives. It would contribute to the Single Market by facilitating the free movement of citizens and goods, enhance the EU’s energy and transport resilience, and create high-value employment opportunities.

* Reducing the dependence on short-haul flights among European major cities is

  essential in order to achieve the EU's climate neutrality objective set for the year 2050\. The expansion of the European High-speed Rail should be a priority

  for sustainable mobility.

* Rail infrastructure investment strengthens economic cohesion, supports the green

  transition, and enhances Europe’s strategic autonomy

* Standardising core rail hardware and operations is essential to unlock true interoperability, accessibility, and network capacity across the Union.

* A truly European rail system, with unified standards and open competition, embodies the freedom of movement and cooperation that define the European

  project.

* A coherent high-speed rail network can help balance economic development by

  enhancing access to markets, labour, and services across all regions of the Union.

* High-speed rail must benefit all Europeans, not just those living in major cities. Regional and local rail connections must be integrated with high-speed  
  networks, ensuring that people in smaller towns and rural areas can also access

  fast, sustainable transport without being left behind.

LYMEC calls for:

* Full implementation of ERTMS and completion of the TEN-T core and comprehensive

  networks by 2040, achieving true interoperability across the European rail system—covering high-speed, regional, and freight lines—to ensure safe, efficient, and sustainable cross-border travel.

* Harmonization of national railway regulations and technical standards.

  Enabling

  the Single European Area where high-speed trains can operate seamlessly across

  Member States borders.

* Adaption of English as the common operational language for cross-border rail,  
  with EU-wide training, standard phraseology and proficiency assessment to maintain safety.  
* Reinforcement of the ERA by expanding its mandate to oversee technical standardization, safety certification, and cross-border project coordination, ensuring consistent implementation of EU railway legislation across Member States.  
* Union-wide harmonisation of the platform-train interface and station servicing standards, plus a coordinated migration to Digital Automatic Coupling in freight.  
* The creation of interoperable digital ticketing standards for European rail, ensuring affordable and transparent access for all passengers, particularly young people, while maintaining an open and competitive market.

* The establishment of a common European digital rail platform integrating timetables, real-time data, and cross-operator ticketing systems, promoting

open

data standards and full interoperability between national operators.

* ETCS by default on new/modernised infrastructure and onboard for all newly authorised rolling stock, prioritising cross-border corridors.  
* Increased accountability and time-bound progress requirements for all projects  
  involving the construction or modernization of infrastructure that forms part of

  the European rail network, ensuring that Member States and operators meet agreed objectives within defined timeframes to prevent delays and cost overruns.

* Governmental and EU-level support for multimodal transport initiatives, such as  
  Rail+Sail connections and integrated transport hubs, to improve sustainable interconnectivity across Europe’s transport networks and to allow interconnectivity with islands.

* Strategic development of connections between high-speed rail lines and regional

  train networks, enabling passengers from smaller cities and rural areas to reach

  the European rail system without depending on cars to get to and from stations.
