(Passed Autumn 2024\)

MO’s supporting: Unge Venstre (Norway), Alliance Youth (Northern Ireland), Keskustan Opiskelijaliitto (Finland), Keskustanuoret (Finland), LiDeM (North Macedonia), Venstres Ungdom (Denmark), YMRF (Bulgaria), Silvia Fernández (IMS), Liberala Ungdomsförbundet (Sweden), Jonge Democraten (Netherlands), Svensk Ungdom (Finland), Radikal Ungdom (Denmark), Centerpartiets Ungdomsförbund (Sweden), Jonk Demokraten (Luxembourg), Youth Forum of Naša stranka (Bosnia and Herzegovina)

Noting that:

* Since the Taliban seized power in August 2021, women’s rights in Afghanistan have regressed dramatically, undoing decades of progress toward gender equality.

* The European Court of Justice (ECJ) recently ruled that gender and nationality alone are sufficient reasons to grant asylum to Afghan women. Currently, some European countries, such as Bulgaria, provide first-instance protection to as few as 12% of Afghan asylum seekers despite the severity of their situation.

* In Afghanistan over 70 decrees have been enacted during the last three years, excluding women and girls from nearly all aspects of public life and leading to what is being termed as "gender apartheid."

* Girls are banned from schooling beyond 6th grade, and women are barred from most paid employment as well as public spaces such as parks and gyms. They are forced to wear full-body coverings and cover their faces. Women are not even allowed to speak in public any more.

* More than one million school-aged girls—80%—are out of school, with dire consequences for future generations, including a projected 50% increase in early childbearing and maternal mortality.

* In 2023, 28.7% of Afghan girls under 18 were married and 9.6% under the age of 15\.

* The Taliban have openly stated that the ideal situation is for women to avoid leaving their homes entirely, unless absolutely necessary. This forced isolation is driving many women and girls into deep despair with increasing suicide attempts.

* The Taliban's new self-proclaimed ‘Ministry of Virtue and Vice’ has tortured women protesters with electric shocks, with reports of rape in custody, and their highest leader has announced the reintroduction of public flogging and stoning for adultery.

* Believing that:

* The brutality of the Taliban’s rule mandates the European countries to act immediately and protect Afghan women that escaped the regime.

* The rise of anti-refugee rhetoric in Europe \- also amongst ALDE member parties

  \- ridicules the serious reality of Afghan refugees and counteracts integration efforts whilst it strengthens the European far-right and nationalist forces.

Therefore, LYMEC calls for:

* Immediate improvement of first-instance protection for Afghan women in Europe.

* ALDE members to stand firm against fundamentalism and the far right, recognize the harsh reality of women that escaped Afghanistan and return to a rhetoric that promotes the open society and a moral Europe.

* Targeted sanctions against Taliban leaders responsible for human rights abuses, and support for international legal accountability.

* A strong stance by EU countries in international forums, such as the United Nations, condemning the abuse of Afghan women’s rights.

* Increased efforts to return Afghan girls to school.

* Avoidance of using Taliban terminology, such as the "Ministry of Virtue."

* Gender Equality Initiatives, in the context of feminist development cooperation, should be an important pillar of international aid to Afghanistan. Donors who engage in development cooperation with Afghanistan need to ensure that their projects don't fund the Taliban regime.
