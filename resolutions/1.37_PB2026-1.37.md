**The Assembly,**

*Considering That:*

*European defense industry and strategic autonomy:*

* A strong European defense industry is crucial for strategic autonomy, reducing dependence on non-European actors, and securing the continent’s long-term stability.

* The European Defense Fund (EDF) was created to enhance the EU’s security and defense capabilities by funding research and development in the defense sector. However, its accessibility and impact remain limited due to administrative complexity and lack of private sector engagement.

* European startups and SMEs specializing in defense technologies face significant financial and bureaucratic barriers, limiting their potential to contribute to a stronger European defense sector.  
* While third-country investments in European defense companies have increased, this raises concerns over strategic dependencies and the protection of sensitive technologies.

*Military preparedness and security situation:*

* Current Defense Expenditure: European nations’ defense spending averages around 1.5% of GDP, below the NATO-recommended 2%, highlighting the need for increased investment.

* To ensure adequate defense, Europe may need to recruit an additional 300,000 troops, addressing both quantity and specialized skill gaps.  
* If U.S. military support were to decrease, Europe would face major capability gaps in air defense, logistics, and intelligence, which require urgent investment.  
* The European Court of Auditors’ Special report 04/2025 highlights significant delays in the EU’s military mobility, which is hindering rapid deployment of armed forces across member states.  
* Despite allocating 1,7 billion euros to 95 projects across 21 member states, Europe’s military mobility remains sluggish, emphasizing the need for more efficient investments in improving the overall mobility.

*Economic considerations:*

* Defense innovation and industry growth are key drivers of economic development, reducing unemployment and brain drain by creating highly skilled jobs in technology, engineering, and security sectors.

* Europe’s defense industry is fragmented, leading to costly inefficiencies and reliance on non-European suppliers for critical military equipment.

* Civil clauses at several European universities severely limit the application of academic research results by restricting their usage exclusively to the civilian sector.

*Believing that:*

*European investments and strategic autonomy:*

* A robust European defense sector is a pillar of sovereignty, economic resilience, and technological advancement.  
* Encouraging private investment in defense industries, alongside EDF support, will strengthen the EU’s industrial base and foster innovation.  
* Public-private partnerships are essential to advancing defense innovation and ensuring Europe remains competitive in high-tech security and military sectors.  
* Investing in the defense industry can contribute to youth employment, providing opportunities for European talent to remain and thrive in the region instead of seeking employment elsewhere and having a brain drain.  
* A robust and self-reliant European defense capability is crucial for the continent’s sovereignty and security.

*Private sector and economic benefits:*

* A coordinated European approach is necessary to address industry gaps, develop a sustainable defense ecosystem, and counteract external influence on strategic sectors.

* Simplifying funding procedures and increasing transparency in the EDF would ensure that more companies, particularly SMEs and startups—can benefit from European support.  
* Investments in defense can stimulate economic growth, create jobs, and drive technological advancements with civilian applications.  
* Engaging private enterprises, especially SMEs and startups, can foster innovation and address specific defense needs.  
* Promoting defense-related education and career paths can mitigate brain drain and retain talent within Europe.

*Military mobility:*

* Efficient military mobility across Europe is essential for its strategic autonomy and rapid response capabilities.  
* Streamlined procedures and infrastructure enhancements are vital to facilitate swift movement of military personnel and equipment across borders

* Enhanced oversight and coordination between EU institutions and member states are crucial for effective implementation of military mobility projects.

*Concludes that:*

*Policy and investment measures:*

* Awareness must be raised on the economic and security benefits of investing in European defense industries.

* Cooperation between member states, private investors, and European institutions should be strengthened to ensure a coherent defense investment strategy that prioritizes military mobility.

* Simplified and faster access to the European Defense Fund is needed to maximize its impact, particularly for SMEs and startups working on defense-related innovation.  
* The defense industry should be positioned as a key sector for employment and technological growth, with initiatives to attract young professionals.

*Defense spending and production acceleration:*

* Efforts should be made to streamline the European defense industry, reducing fragmentation and enhancing production capabilities  
* European countries should increase military stockpiles and accelerate production capacity for key equipment, ensuring rapid mobilization in case of conflict.  
* European nations should spend 3.5% to 4% of GDP on defense to adequately address current security challenges, with a portion dedicated to improving military harmonization.  
* Recruitment drives should be initiated to increase military personnel by approximately 300,000, focusing on both combat and technical roles.  
* Significant resources should be allocated to research and development in emerging defense technologies, such as AI, unmanned systems, and cybersecurity.

*Calls for:*

*Policy and investment reforms:*

* European governments to facilitate private investment in defense industries by providing financial incentives, reducing bureaucratic hurdles, and ensuring a stable regulatory framework.

* Partial tax exemptions for European companies investing in defense R\&D, ensuring sustained innovation while reducing reliance on external funding sources.

* A structured regulatory framework for public-private partnerships in defense, allowing corporations to invest in military R\&D with incentives while ensuring national security oversight.

* The institutionalization of a European Defense Technology Accelerator, modeled after the U.S. DARPA, to push cutting-edge defense innovation forward.

* The institutionalization of a European framework for defense sector transparency, ensuring accountability in investments while maintaining security priorities.  
* The European Commission to reform EDF procedures to make funding more accessible, particularly for SMEs and startups engaged in defense research and development.  
* The creation of a European Defense Investment Board composed of policymakers, industry leaders, and defense experts to monitor investments, guide industrial strategies, and safeguard Europe’s strategic interests.  
* The establishment of a European Defense Industrial Strategy, reducing reliance on third-country suppliers and fostering intra-EU cooperation in military production.

* The abolition of civil clauses at European universities, thereby enabling research results to equally benefit the civilian and military sector.

*Industrial capabilities and innovation:*

* The acceleration of joint procurement programs to develop and deploy next-generation European-made fighter jets, armored vehicles, and naval assets.

* The expansion of military stockpiles for ammunition, fuel, and critical spare parts to ensure readiness in prolonged conflicts.

* European governments to increase defense spending to a minimum of 3.5% of GDP, ensuring sustainable long-term investments in strategic defense capabilities.

* European governments should work together on a comprehensive strategy for purchasing large quantities of new equipment to enable all armed forces to operate efficiently with each other in times of crisis.

* The creation of a European Defense Innovation Fund to finance startups, SMEs, and research institutions working on advanced military technologies.

* The promotion of dual-use technologies that serve both civilian and military applications, increasing market competitiveness and innovation within the sector.  
* The establishment of industry-academia partnerships to provide scholarships, internships, and training programs that encourage young professionals to enter the defense sector.

*Intelligence and military efficiency:*

* The development of an EU-wide security clearance system to facilitate the exchange of classified intelligence between member states and defense companies.

* A long-term recruitment and training plan for expanding Europe’s defense personnel, ensuring the armed forces have access to skilled engineers, cyber experts, and AI specialists.

*Enhanced military mobility:*

* Improved coordination among EU institutions, member states, and NATO to align military mobility initiatives with broader defense strategies.

* Investing in infrastructure projects that are dual-use, serving both civilian and military purposes to maximise investment efficiency and military logistic mobility.  
* Standardizing cross-border military transport procedures to reduce delays caused by different national regulations on military mobility.  
* Establishing clear guidelines for the movement of military personnel and equipment across member states, minimizing bureaucratic obstacles.

* Conducting regular joint military exercises that are specifically focused on testing our European military mobility and using its results in making further improvements to military mobility in Europe possible.

* Developing training programs for military logistics personnel to increase their capabilities in executing rapid deployment and movement of military personnel and equipment.
