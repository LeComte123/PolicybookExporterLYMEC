***Passed Spring 2026***

Movers: Bundesverband Liberaler Hochschulgruppen, Young Green Liberals, Alliance Youth, Silvia Fernandez (IMS), Arthur Wu (IMS), representing the European Liberal Student Network/Education Policy Working Group.

Noting that:

* Disinformation campaigns are on the rise across Europe, originating not only from systemic rivals and foreign state actors but also from anti-democratic movements within Member States.

* Such campaigns aim to undermine public confidence in democratic institutions, electoral processes, scientific expertise, and independent journalism.

* Digital media platforms, while essential for free expression, can facilitate the rapid spread of manipulated or misleading information, especially among younger audiences.

* Existing national education systems often lack effective approaches to media and digital literacy, leaving citizens more vulnerable to manipulation.

* Traditional news outlets face persistent challenges in engaging the public, including declining audience engagement, the erosion of trust, and stagnant digital subscription growth, undermining their ability to effectively counter disinformation as highlighted by the 2025 Digital News Report.

Considering that:

* The spread of disinformation and misinformation poses a growing threat to democratic processes, social cohesion, and citizens’ trust in institutions across Europe.

* Critical-thinking skills and media literacy remain underdeveloped amongst citizens in many Member States, leaving them more vulnerable to manipulation and false narratives.

* Finland has successfully implemented comprehensive media literacy education as part of its national curriculum, serving as a proven model for fellow European countries.

* Artificial intelligence is oftentimes used as a primary source of information, but it can be biased and respond incorrectly.

* A high proportion of youth regularly use social media, for example 6 in 10 teens use TikTok as highlighted in the Pew Research Centre Report.

* The Trump administration has cut the Corporation for Global Media and is cutting off funds to entities such as Radio Free Europe (RFE).

* The EU has already provided emergency funding to RFE in 2025\.

Believing that:

* A strong and resilient Europe depends on informed citizens, capable of distinguishing facts from manipulation and engaging critically in democratic debate.

* Media literacy and critical thinking skills are fundamental liberal democratic values that empower informed citizenship and strengthen citizens’ ability to engage responsibly in democratic discourse.

* Access to quality information from free press is necessary in order to counter the threat from authoritarian adversaries, such as Russia, in the dissemination of factual information.

* Strengthening education and media literacy is the most sustainable long-term strategy to counter disinformation whilst fully safeguarding freedom of expression and the free exchange of ideas that underpin liberal democracy, without resorting to censorship or restrictions on legitimate speech.

* Despite EU intervention as described below, significant gaps remain in addressing the rapid evolution of AI-driven threats.

Acknowledging that:

* The European Union has recognised the challenge of disinformation, notably through the European Democracy Action Plan and the European Democracy Shield.

* The EU Digital Education Action Plan (2021-2027) identifies media literacy as a key priority but implementation remains inconsistent across Member States.

Notwithstanding that:

* Combatting disinformation must never come at the expense of freedom of expression, media independence, or pluralism of opinion, as guaranteed by Article 11 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union.

LYMEC calls for:

* EU Member States to integrate media and digital literacy, critical thinking, and civic education as core elements of their primary and secondary school curricula.

* EU Member States to invest in comprehensive teacher training and provide adequate resources following Finland’s example, including regularly updated teaching materials and digital tools, to strengthen educators’ ability to train students to recognise and critically assess false or misleading information.

* EU Member States and the European Commission to develop awareness campaigns that strengthen media and information literacy among adults and senior citizens, ensuring that efforts to counter disinformation extend beyond schools and reach all generations of society.

* Private and public sector employers to integrate AI literacy training in the workplace to empower employees in detecting and countering AI-generated disinformation, creating a more resilient and better informed society.

* The Commission to ensure the long-term sustainment of independent quality broadcasters through mechanisms like funding schemes or resource support, in order to safeguard independent journalism as a natural defence against disinformation.

* The European Union to facilitate dedicated training for independent journalists and established media outlets to strengthen fact-checking, pre-bunking, and other measures countering disinformation, including AI-generated manipulation.
