**Passed Spring 2026**

Considering that:

* PWhilst 22 European states recognise same sex marriages, over half of all European states do not.

* Other regulated forms of partnerships, such as civil unions or registered partnerships, are available to same-sex couples in several member states, but often do not afford the same scope of rights as marriage.

* Some member states do not legally recognize same-sex relationships at all, and in some member, states including some which allow for civil unions, the constitution limits marriage to different-sex couples.

* Several European states who are currently applying for EU membership such as Ukraine, Montenegro and Türkiye fail either wholly or in part to provide legal recognition for same sex marriages and unions.

* Discrimination on the basis of sexual orientation is incompatible with liberal values.

* For many, the concept of marriage stills holds a particular cultural importance.

* The recent judgements in Fedotova v. Russia and Przybyszewska v. Poland have established that all European states owe an obligation to same sex couples to protect and recognise their legal partnerships.

* The free movement of individuals is at the core of the founding values of the EU.

* The right to privacy and a family life is protected by the European Convention on Human Rights

LYMEC believes:

* All Europeans must be able to exercise their rights regardless of their sexual orientation.

* Any attempt by the state to restrict who can marry who is an illegal attack on those rights.

* That subscribing to those principles should be a prerequisite for joining and participating fully in the European Union and other European bodies.

* Same-sex couples who have children through surrogacy, donors and other means deserve access to maternity and paternity leave, pay and rights.

LYMEC calls for:

* All EU member states to recognize same-sex couples who have married or entered in to a civil union, cohabitation or similar arrangement in other states, even if the member state does not itself perform such marriages or arrangements. The concerned arrangements should retain the same legal protection in all member states.

* All EU member states to introduce legislation to introduce equal rights for same-sex couples, including but not limited to recognising and allowing for same sex marriages and unions, affording those marriages and unions the same legal, familial rights as a heterosexual marriage or union, and removing any constitutional or legal discrimination against same sex couples

* The EU to introduce a chapter in to its Acquis communautaire requiring candidate states to prove that they will afford same-sex couples completely equal rights within a brief period of time after accession to the union, and an obligation to provide basic rights such as recognition of foreign same sex marriages and unions, the introduction of civil unions if not already in place, and the removal of all laws that explicitly forbid same sex marriages, unions and relationships.

* All European states that do not currently afford same-Sex couples equal rights to outline and pursue an immediate legislative pathway to satisfying their legal obligation to same sex couples

* All LYMEC members in states which do not currently afford same sex couples equal rights to lobby their mother party and others to change the law
