Considering that:

- a competitive European pharmaceutical sector is essential to public health, scientific progress, skilled employment and Europe’s ability to respond to health crises;
- In 2022, the European pharmaceutical industry contributed €311 billion to the EU’s gross value added and generated approximately 2.3 million direct and indirect jobs ;
- Each employee in the pharmaceutical sector generates on average €225,300 in value added, according to PwC ;
- In 2024, EU pharmaceutical exports to third countries totalled €313.4 billion, underscoring the global competitiveness of the sector ;
- The COVID-19 crisis demonstrated that a strong pharmaceutical sector is essential for the EU’s strategic autonomy and sovereignty ;
- European companies, including BioNTech, played a pioneering role in developing and producing mRNA vaccines — with industrial production largely taking place within the EU (Germany, Belgium, France, the Netherlands) ;
- Over 70 % of vaccines produced in the EU were exported to more than 160 countries, making the Union a global production hub ;
- EU public funds directly supported research, development, and industrial scale-up during the pandemic ;
- Pharmaceutical leadership requires an environment in which research can develop into viable businesses, innovative treatments and reliable manufacturing capacity;
- concentrated supply chains and dependence on a limited number of suppliers can expose patients and healthcare systems to shortages and external disruption;
- international trade and scientific cooperation support pharmaceutical innovation, while diversified partnerships strengthen Europe’s resilience;
- gaps in understanding women’s health and sex-related differences in treatment responses represent both unmet medical needs and an important frontier for European research and innovation;
- industrial success must translate into measurable improvements in the availability, safety, effectiveness and affordability of medicines;

Noting that:

- the EU’s framework for clinical trials and the Clinical Trials Information System provide a basis for multinational research and transparent reporting;
- European initiatives concerning critical medicines address the relationship between manufacturing capacity, diversified supply chains and security of supply;
- differences in the absorption, distribution, metabolism and elimination of medicines can affect treatment responses, making appropriate investigation of sex-related differences relevant to pharmaceutical development and safety;
- women’s health extends beyond reproductive health and requires attention across therapeutic fields and throughout life;
- universities, hospitals, pharmaceutical companies and biotechnology SMEs have complementary roles in translating scientific discoveries into treatments;

Believing that:

- Europe should aspire to global pharmaceutical leadership through scientific excellence, entrepreneurship, open competition and predictable regulation;
- strengthening European capabilities should go together with international cooperation and diversified trade;
- addressing unmet needs in women’s health should be a strategic research and investment priority, with clear objectives and accountable funding;
- scientific inclusion requires research populations and analyses appropriate to the condition and intended treatment population, with therapeutic decisions grounded in evidence;
- public support should address identified needs, attract additional investment and deliver demonstrable public value;
- innovation, patient safety and access to treatment are mutually reinforcing objectives;

LYMEC calls for:

1\. Strengthening European pharmaceutical competitiveness and commercial leadership by:

- calling on EU institutions and Member States, within their respective competences, to provide predictable regulatory and investment conditions for pharmaceutical research, development and manufacturing;
- simplifying duplicative administrative procedures and improving coordination between authorities while preserving scientific, ethical and safety standards;
- facilitating access to competitive research funding, growth capital and regulatory advice for European startups, SMEs and biotechnology companies;
- supporting partnerships between universities, hospitals and businesses to bring discoveries through clinical development and into production;
- maintaining effective intellectual property protection alongside competition and timely entry of generic and biosimilar medicines in accordance with applicable rules;
- pursuing trade agreements and regulatory cooperation that reduce unnecessary barriers, diversify export markets and preserve European health standards;
- The European Commission to seek the elimination or substantial reduction of tariffs and non-tariff barriers for European pharmaceutical products exported to important markets.

2\. Building resilient European manufacturing and medicine supplies by:

- identifying critical medicines, active ingredients and production stages exposed to significant supply vulnerabilities;
- directing transparent, proportionate and regularly reviewed support towards these vulnerabilities, combining European investment with diversified international sourcing;
- strengthening coordinated shortage monitoring, early notification and contingency planning between competent authorities, manufacturers and distributors;
- developing risk-based stock arrangements that consider shelf life, patient needs and cross-border solidarity;
- incorporating quality, supply reliability and continuity into public procurement, including multiple suppliers where appropriate;
- ensuring that resilience measures remain compatible with competition and avoid creating new dependencies on individual companies or suppliers;

3\. Making women’s health a strategic pillar of pharmaceutical innovation by:

- establishing a clearly identified women’s health research and innovation priority within existing European funding programmes, with dedicated competitive calls, transparent budgets and measurable objectives;
- supporting research into conditions specific to women, disproportionately affecting them or presenting differently in women, including endometriosis, polycystic ovary syndrome and sex-related differences in cardiovascular disease and treatment responses;
- strengthening the translation of this research into validated diagnostics, medicines and treatment strategies through partnerships between researchers, clinicians, patients and businesses;
- facilitating access to finance and scientific advice for companies developing solutions to unmet needs in women’s health, subject to the same standards of clinical evidence and safety as other innovations;
- requiring publicly funded clinical trials to justify recruitment against the intended treatment population, explain exclusions and prespecify sex-related analyses where scientifically relevant, with adequate statistical planning;
- supporting research methods that appropriately account for age, hormonal status and other relevant biological factors, while avoiding unjustified blanket exclusions;
- strengthening the analysis of medicine effectiveness and adverse reactions by sex, and requiring reassessment of dosage, product information or clinical recommendations when sufficiently robust evidence warrants it;
- publishing progress on research funding, trial participation, evidence gaps and resulting therapeutic advances, while protecting personal data and research participants;

4\. Turning pharmaceutical leadership into better access and accountable results by:

- improving coordination between regulatory assessment, health technology assessment and national access and reimbursement processes, while respecting their distinct responsibilities;
- promoting transparent decisions based on clinical benefit, unmet medical needs and the sustainability of healthcare systems;
- Encouraging Member States to jointly negotiate the prices of expensive medicines with pharmaceutical companies, while promoting greater transparency regarding research, development and production costs, to strengthen their bargaining power and ensure affordable access to innovative treatments across Europe;
- supporting professional training in pharmaceutical research, manufacturing, regulatory science and the assessment of sex-related differences;
- enabling secure and scientifically validated uses of health data and digital tools, with effective privacy and cybersecurity safeguards;
- calling on the European Commission, together with Member States, to publish within twelve months of taking up these recommendations a costed implementation roadmap identifying responsible authorities, funding sources and milestones;
- assessing progress annually across investment, manufacturing resilience, shortages, SME growth, women’s health research and patient access, with an independent review after three years;
