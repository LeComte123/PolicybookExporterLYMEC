(Passed Spring 2025\)

Secure the Eastern Flank: Secure the Union

Submitted by: USR Tineret, Liberal Democratic League of Ukraine, Nowoczesna Youth Co-signed by: JUNOS, Arthur Wu (IMS), Young Progressive Slovakia, European Youth of Ukraine, Adam Volf (IMS), Young Liberals, Molodezhka, Attistibai Youth, Alliance Youth, Lithuanian Liberal Youth, Jonge Democraten, Youth Forum of Naša stranka, Jongliberalen, Junge Liberale, JOVD

*Considering that:*

* The European Union faces a growing security threat from external adversaries, including China, Russia, Iran, and North Korea. Russia's continued aggression, exemplified by its war in Ukraine, challenges regional stability, while China's economic and cyber influence poses strategic risks. Iran's support for proxy groups and its deepening ties with Russia further destabilize the security landscape. Additionally, North Korean troops have reportedly been deployed near the EU's eastern border, escalating concerns over military threats;  
* Eastern European countries are the first line of defence against Russian aggression, as well as under the most significant and immediate threat of both hybrid and conventional attacks;  
* Russia has openly demanded the re-establishment of NATO’s borders to a pre-2004 configuration, posing a direct threat to the sovereignty and security of Eastern European nations and undermining the stability of the region;  
* Conflicts, external interferences and general instability in Eastern Europe directly affects all of Europe;  
* The European continent is not only affected by conventional attacks, but also by hybrid warfare tactics. Eastern European countries are particularly targeted by these hybrid threats, which remain largely unaddressed;

* Europe has seen a surge of disinformation campaigns, especially focused on raising ultra nationalist, isolationist and revanchist sentiments, aimed at compromising European security and solidarity from within;

* The omnipresent foreign disinformation, misinformation and propaganda are undermining Europe’s firm support for Ukraine;

* The phenomenon of isolationism is perilously becoming the new core of the Euro-Atlantic policy of the United States of America \- Europe’s most crucial ally;

* Outside of the European Union, Europe also is home to three other prominent NATO members \- the United Kingdom, Norway and Türkiye

* Whilst the European Union is home to several of the world's most innovative and prestigious defence firms, only two EU based contractors are in the world's top 30 largest defence firms.

*Believing that:*

* In 2025, we are witnessing powerful international actors engaging in politics meant to change the rules-based international order which existed from WW2 onwards;  
* Traditional partners of European countries engaging in bullying behavior is a strong signal that Europeans should further consolidate their own common security;

* There is virtually and literally no chance of European countries tackling security challenges each on their own, and challenges present themselves in a rather near than distant future;

* In a world where powerful international actors are engaging in politics meant to change the rules-based international order, there is a grave and immediate threat to European democracy and to European liberalism;  
* External actors are pursuing an aggressive disinformation campaign against the European Union and its Member States, bordering on actions of hybrid warfare, in order to delegitimize their institutions, attack their common values, weaken their sense of solidarity and undermine European security;

* The treaties on Good-Neighbourliness, Friendly Relations, and Cooperation signed between former Warsaw Pact member states following the fall of the Iron Curtain were essential instruments for maintaining regional stability, fostering diplomatic cooperation, and promoting economic and social ties. These treaties serve as a foundation for conflict prevention, minority rights protection, and peaceful coexistence in Eastern Europe;  
* Relying too heavily upon non-European security firms and externally sourced intelligence compromises Europe’s ability to defend itself;  
* The European Union is the world’s second largest economy and, thus, must be capable of defending itself, especially in case of military aggression;  
* Eastern European security is European security;

* A divided Europe is a weak Europe.

*Welcomes:*

* The EU’s recent “ReArm Europe” plan;

* The efforts made by European countries in supporting Ukraine obtain a just and lasting peace;

* The 12 Joint Expeditionary Force (JEF) Members commitment to collaboration in keeping the Eastern Flank of Europe safe through joint exercises and intelligence sharing.

*Calls for:*

* All EU Member States to reaffirm their commitment to the mutual defence clause of the European Union (Article 42.7 of the Treaty of the European Union);

* The strengthening of joint military-technological cooperation, security and innovation; the European Union must prioritize collaborative defence research, weapons development, and cybersecurity advancements. This effort should integrate lessons learned from the battlefield experience of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, ensuring that emerging military technologies are battle-tested, adaptable, and capable of countering evolving threats;  
* European countries to up their budgetary allowances towards defence, be it into the military, cybersecurity, critical infrastructure or technology which could serve for deterrence and defence purposes;  
* The EU to urgently allocate funds and create funding mechanisms to be used for defence spending and investment, especially towards bolstering the security of the Union’s Eastern Flank;  
* European countries to ensure that Ukraine and the rest of the European allies have access to high-level military intelligence and counterintelligence resources, and that their intelligence and counterintelligence efforts are developed in collaboration with trustworthy non-European and international partners who share their values.  
* The European Union to consider further advanced cooperation with democratic partners and strategic allies sharing mutual international values.  
* All EU member states to compliment the joint procurement strategy of ‘ReArm’ through encouraging defence and security firms to further collaborate across borders, and consider consolidating into larger companies;  
* Eastern European countries to enhance regional cooperation in order to address present and future security threats. This should include not only conventional defence but also prevention of cyber attacks, political and electoral interference, energy shortages and any other areas necessary for maintaining the peace and security of the region;  
* European countries in general and (non-aggressor) Eastern European countries in particular to stand together against foreign actors trying to pit them against one another. Any and all attempts to divide or isolate us need to be met with united, unwavering opposition;  
* European countries which are not currently JEF members to explore funding and collaboration with JEF in order to help deter any potential Russian aggression along Europe's North-Eastern borders;

* ALDE and its member parties to condemn any and all attempts to sabotage friendly relations and cooperation between (non-aggressor) Eastern European countries and reaffirm support for good-neighbourliness, friendly relations, and cooperation in the region;

* The B9 (Bucharest Nine) countries to integrate Ukraine into the format;

* All European countries to continue to support Ukraine;

* The European Commission to allocate additionals funds for Poland’s East Shield initiative and other similar Eastern Flank defence projects;

* All European nations to raise defence spending over the 2% of GDP target, starting 2026\.
