Remembering That

* Greenland has been an official part of the Kingdom of Denmark since 1814, following the Treaty of Kiel.  
*  As the former colonial power in Greenland, Denmark's historical role continues to shape the relationship between the two countries and forms part of the backdrop for ongoing Greenlandic aspirations for greater self-determination.  
* Greenland achieved expanded self-governance in 2009, granting its government authority over domestic affairs while Denmark retains responsibility for foreign affairs, defence, and monetary policy.  
* Denmark and the United States are longstanding allies, and have worked together on defence and security for decades, including the 1951 Defence Agreement governing the American military presence in Greenland.  
* Greenland is not a member of the European Union, while Denmark is a full EU member state.  
* Greenland, as an autonomous territory of the Kingdom of Denmark, falls under NATO’s collective defence framework.

Noting That 

* A majority of Greenlanders, and all political parties in Greenland have expressed a desire for independence as a long term goal, and that the decision of independence is a greenlandic prerogative.   
* Economic development is a precondition for a functioning Greenland, independent of the Danish block grant. In this context, Greenland’s natural resources are becoming increasingly important for economic resilience, industrial policy, and strategic autonomy.  
* U.S. President Donald Trump has repeatedly expressed his desire and intention to acquire Greenland one way or another, citing national security interests, while Greenlandic and Danish leaders have rejected any notion that the territory is for sale.  
* The Arctic is of growing strategic importance due to its role in military positioning, maritime routes, space-related infrastructure, critical raw materials, and broader great-power competition.  
* There has been an US military presence in Greenland since 1941, which was of significant strategic importance for Europe throughout the Cold War.  
* Authoritarian powers, like Russia and the People´s Republic of China (PRC) have  articulated a strategic interest in the Arctic and more specifically Greenland and Svalbard.

Believing That 

* The right to self-determination is a fundamental liberal principle, and the Greenlandic people must be allowed to determine their own future without coercion from any foreign power.  
* Any change in Greenland's political status must be driven exclusively by the democratic will of the Greenlandic people.  
* The aggressive rhetoric used by the United States with respect to Greenland is a breach of the principles of rule-based order.  
* Arctic security must be based on cooperation, rules-based order, democratic alliances, and respect for sovereignty, rather than territorial expansion, coercion, and great-power pressure.  
* Greenland’s geopolitical importance extends far beyond the question of formal sovereignty: the Arctic is increasingly central to European and transatlantic security.  
* In a time of increasing competition over critical raw materials, shipping routes, and strategic infrastructure, Europe must not leave Arctic policy to unilateral action by outside powers, but must develop a coherent and responsible approach that combines security, sustainability, and respect for the rights of Greenlanders.

 

LYMEC Calls For

* Institutional investors, such as the European Investment Fund and the European Investment Bank, to invest in Greenland, with the aim of supporting its economic sustainability and greater economic independence away from the Danish bloc grant.  
* National governments and European institutions to unequivocally condemn any military or economic threats against Greenland's territorial integrity, and be ready to back this up with concrete actions if necessary.   
* The United States to respect the democratic and sovereign rights of Greenland.  
* For the European Union to actively promote Greenland's potential route toward membership of the European Union, if the people of Greenland so choose.   
* The Danish government to acknowledge and take steps to rectify the injustices of Danish colonialism in Greenland.  
* The European Union to strengthen its strategic commitment in the Arctic through greater investment in security, infrastructure, research, and diplomatic presence, in order to reduce reliance on unilateral action by outside powers and to contribute to regional stability.  
* The European Union and its member states to support fair, sustainable, and locally beneficial development of Greenland’s natural resources, in ways that strengthen Greenland’s economic self-determination and reduce European dependencies on authoritarian or non-European suppliers.  
* A European military presence on Greenland, which complements the strategic value of Pittufik Space Base, and which safeguards European strategic interests in the Arctic against infringements from authoritarian adversaries such as Russia and the PRC.
