(Former 9.48 Political Situation in Venezuela Prior to Warsaw, Autumn 2024\)

**Advocating for Democracy and Human Rights in Venezuela**

This resolution archives and replaces 9.48

Cosignatories: Joventut Nacionalista de Catalunya (JNC), Ógra Fianna Fáil, Junge Liberale (JuLis), Unge Venstre (NUV), Youth Movement for Rights and Freedoms, Silvia Fernández (IMS), Adam Volf (IMS), Bundesverband Liberaler Hochschulgruppen (LHG), Young Liberals, Svensk Ungdom, Jungfreisinnige Schweiz, Lithuanian Liberal Youth, Radikal Ungdom (RU), Jongeren Organisatie Vrijheid en Democratie (JOVD).

On July 28, 2024, Venezuela held presidential elections under the oversight of the National Electoral Council (CNE), which declared Nicolás Maduro the winner with 51.95% of the vote against opposition candidate Edmundo González, who reportedly received 43.18%. However, the opposition and international observers, including the Carter Center, documented widespread electoral fraud, asserting that González actually won with 67.08% of the vote.  
For 25 years, Chavismo has eroded democratic institutions, engaged in repression, and led Venezuela into severe economic and social crises, prompting one of Latin America’s largest exoduses and widespread calls for change. In response to the alleged fraudulent results, the Venezuelan opposition launched mass mobilizations demanding transparency and democratic transition, with international support.

LYMEC notes that:

* The Chavista regime has repeatedly undermined the rule of law, most notably in 2004 when 12 pro-regime magistrates were added to the Supreme Court. Between 2015 and 2017, the Court stripped the National Assembly of its powers, replacing it with the National Constituent Assembly. This marked a breakdown of both the rule of law and Venezuelan democracy.

* Venezuela has been under Chavista rule since 1999, during which time widespread economic and political repression has led to hyperinflation, significant emigration and corruption, especially in the country's armed forces and in the state-owned oil company, PDVSA.

* Despite the initial international hope for a change of government after 25 years, the Venezuelan opposition and international observers denounced electoral fraud by the Chavista regime, claiming that Gonzalez actually obtained 67.08% of the votes.

* In response to these fraudulent results, the Venezuelan opposition, supported by international organizations, initiated continuous popular mobilizations in favor of transparent electoral results.

LYMEC believes:

* The Chavista regime’s history of expropriating private property, repressing political opponents, and eroding judicial independence is a threat to democracy and human rights.  
* The Venezuelan people deserve transparent elections and a peaceful democratic transition.

* The international community must play a critical role in supporting Venezuela’s democratic opposition and ensuring fair electoral processes.

* Addressing Venezuela’s severe economic and security crises, including the influence of drug trafficking, is vital for achieving sustainable democratic change.

LYMEC calls for:

* Support to the Venezuelan opposition's mobilizations for democracy, including the efforts of the 8 million Venezuelans who have had to flee their country.  
* Immediate cessation of repression of peaceful protests, arbitrary arrests of opposition leaders and persecution of political activists.  
* A continuous monitoring by international bodies, such as the UN Human Rights Council, to ensure accountability for electoral fraud and human rights violations.  
* That the EU and the international community recognize Edmundo Gonzalez as the legitimate elected president and advocate for his peaceful inauguration on January 10, 2025\.  
* Reinstatement of EU sanctions on the Chavista regime until repression is ended, political prisoners are released and accurate election results are published.
