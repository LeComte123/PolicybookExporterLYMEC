**Passed Autumn 2025**

Considering that:

*On Big Data and Data Autonomy*

* Much of our modern lives take place in an online environment;

* Big Data drives much of how online environments function;

* Many data driven algorithms function as black boxes which obfuscates what personal data is used for;

* For many, the online sphere has become their primary source of information, where their personal data is constantly collected and processed;  
* It has become virtually impossible to avoid leaving a digital footprint;

* The data persons leave behind in their digital footprint is deeply valuable to companies, but also to criminals for nefarious purposes such as identity theft

  or identity fraud;

* Data breaches in both governmental institutions and private entities are preventable;

* Dark patterns and manipulative interface designs exploit cognitive biases, undermining genuine consent and user autonomy;

*On Chat Control and Surveillance*

* Extra-judicial monitoring of individuals is in breach of fundamental rights to privacy and data protection;  
* The Child Sexual Abuse Material Regulation colloquially known as Chat Control is  
  put back on the EU agenda by the Danish Presidency of The Council of the European Union;

* Messaging and email services will be mandated to scan all private communications

  as currently proposed in the CSAM Regulation;

* Unknown content that has not been previously identified as illegal can still be flagged using experimental AI as currently proposed in the CSAM Regulation;  
* Services will be mandated to age verify users, effectively ending anonymous communication as currently proposed in the CSAM Regulation;  
* Government and Military communication will be exempted from scanning as currently proposed in the CSAM Regulation;

* Mass surveillance disproportionally affects activists, journalists, and minorities;

* Surveillance measures often end up expanding past their original announced scope

  and purpose;

*On Encryption and Cybersecurity*

* Encryption is a powerful tool that is indispensable and for many industries and sectors to function;

* Much of our finances run through secured electronic channels, which would be

  undermined if encryption is weakened;

* Weakened cybersecurity undermines trust in digital services and governmental

  institutions;

* Technological breakthroughs in quantum computing might end up breaking modern

  advanced encryption methods;

*On Identity Verification and Biometrics*

* The European Commission is working on an age verification scheme for the EU;

* The growing use of identity verification tools make it increasingly important to have a clear legal framework that protects personal data and limits risks like f identity fraud and misuse

* Biometric verification is not alterable after its compromised;

* The United Kingdom Online Safety Act came into effect as of July 2025 which requires all websites hosting erotic content to age verify its users;

*On International Agreements and Data Transfers*

* Global cooperation is growing more uncertain with more and more foreign entities

  turning authoritarian;

* In 2013 WikiLeaks exposed systemic surveillance practices by the US National Security Agency, demonstrating the dangers of unchecked government overreach and  
  the erosion of individual privacy rights;

* These abuses were enabled by an imbalance of power between government

  institutions and citizens, setting a precedent that has since influenced global

  surveillance policies, including those of foreign allies and adversaries;

* Subsequent revelations, such as the 2021 Pegasus Project, have further underscored the global proliferation of surveillance technologies, often deployed without adequate oversight or accountability;

* The normalization of mass surveillance has emboldened authoritarian regimes to

  adopt similar practices, exacerbating risks to human rights and democratic values worldwide;

*On Accountability and Enforcement*

* Many public and private entities fail to provide clear, accessible information about how they collect, use, and share personal data, making it difficult for individuals to hold them accountable;

* High legal costs, complex procedures, and unequal power dynamics between

  individuals and corporations or governments create barriers for victims of privacy violations seeking redress.

Noting that:

*On Big Data and Data Autonomy*

* Personal data shall be kept in a form which permits identification of data subjects for no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal

  data is processed;

* The EU General Data Protection Regulation already took big steps in increasing

  people’s data autonomy. though ensuring consistent compliance and user-friendly

  access to these rights remains a priority;

* The EU Digital Services Act and Digital Markets Act both already address issues

  such as user protection and digital markets;

* The EU Digital Governance Act already addresses and aims to facilitate secure

  data sharing in public and private sectors;

* Due to the often large and complex nature of privacy notices, users grow fatigued, which leads to them regularly blindly accepting them, meaning users

  are seldom sufficiently informed;

* Data is essential to technological development, and is thus needed for private

  entities to thrive;

* The demand and usage of data rapidly changes in time with its continuous technological development, which makes regulation a complex and continuous

  process;

*On Chat Control and Surveillance*

* Article 7 of The Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union directly states: ‘Everyone has the right to respect for his or her private and family life, home and communications.’;

* Article 12 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights of the United Nations directly states: 'No one shall be subjected to arbitrary interference with his privacy, family, home or correspondence...'

* Article 19 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights of the United Nations directly states: 'Everyone has the right to freedom of opinion and expression;

this right includes freedom to hold opinions without interference and to seek,

receive and impart information and ideas through any media and regardless of

frontiers.'

* Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights of the Council of Europe

  directly states: '(1) Everyone has the right to respect for his private and family life, his home and his correspondence.' and '(2) There shall be no interference by a public authority with the exercise of this right except such as is in accordance with the law and is necessary in a democratic society in the

  interests of national security, public safety or the economic well-being of the

  country, for the prevention of disorder or crime, for the protection of health or morals, or for the protection of the rights and freedoms of others.'

* The European Court of Human Rights has consistently interpreted Article 8 of the

  European Convention on Human Rights of the Council of Europe as covering

  personal data protection and the individual's control over information relating

  to them;

* Secrecy of correspondence is a constitutional right in many countries;

* The CSAM Regulation proposal is unambiguously mass surveillance legislation;

* Child protection experts warn the CSAM Regulation proposal would actually be

  counterproductive to child safety;

* Under extraordinary circumstances some tightly targeted and regulated forms of

  surveillance might be justified if it concerns matters such as national security bearing necessity and proportionality in mind;

* Traces of the Pegasus spy software have been found globally, including on the

  phones of French journalists, Bahraini activists, and Indian deputies, and over

  60 Catalan citizens including three Members of the European Parliament, demonstrating its widespread misuse;

*On Encryption and Cybersecurity*

* The CSAM Regulation proposal will break end-to-end encryption exposing everyone’s communication;

* Cybersecurity experts warn weakening encryption will significantly increase the

  risk of highly personal communications leaking to the hands of

cybercriminals

when data breaches inevitably occur;

* If criminal content currently runs through legal encrypted channels, introducing

  a backdoor would simply drive criminals to use illicit mediums;

*On Identity Verification and Biometrics*

* In the event of a biometric data breach, the risk of identity theft,

  surveillance, and misuse is permanent, making biometric breaches far more damaging than traditional data leaks;

* Biometric databases are high-value targets. Breaches can reveal not just identity but also sensitive personal behaviors, amplifying the potential for abuse and long-term harm;

* Hospitals are already often targets for hacking attempt due to the value of the

  compromised data that they store;

* Biometric systems can be spoofed or bypassed using AI or forged materials, making them less secure than often assumed;

*On International Agreements and Data Transfers*

* The EU-US Passenger Name Record agreement is still legally in force; \[1\]

* The EU-US Passenger Name Record agreement is a violation of fundamental citizen’s rights; \[2\]

* Passenger Name Record agreements with countries such as Australia,

  Canada, and

  the UK could or already pose similar threats to citizen’s rights;

* The Court of Justice of the European Union invalidated the EU-US Privacy Shield

  in the Schrems II decision citing amongst other reasons, a lack of necessity and

  proportionality in US surveillance programs; \[3,4\]

*On Accountability and Enforcement*

* The EU already regularly hands out sizable fines to large companies for breaching GDPR rules;  
* Enforcement of privacy laws against large corporations remains inconsistent and  
  many breaches still go insufficiently penalized despite growing regulatory action.

Believing that:

*On Big Data and Data Autonomy*

* The right to privacy is a fundamental right and one of our core values;

* Our fundamental rights of privacy need to be safeguarded for all citizens;

* Every person should have the right to autonomy over their personal data;

* Every person should be sufficiently informed on the data they opt to share and

  which is collected regarding them;

* Internet standards bodies are best-placed to develop technical standards related

  to Internet services and apps;

*On Chat Control and Surveillance*

* The CSAM Regulation proposal is a thinly veiled attempt to introduce mass surveillance legislation under the gist of child protection;

* Anti-crime and anti-terrorism legislation should always bear necessity and proportionality in mind;  
* Citizens should never fear prosecution for proclaiming their ideals, thoughts, and opinions;  
* The CSAM Regulation risks having a chilling effect on freedom of expression in Europe.

* Allowing any form of chat control for any reason risks function and mission creep;

* Introducing such far-reaching measures as the CSAM Regulation carries the risk

  of setting a precedent for further encroachments on individual privacy. As the

  saying goes, “the road to hell is paved with good intentions.”

* Resources should be directed towards targeted, effective law enforcement and

  prevention strategies, not blanket surveillance of private conversations.

* Protecting children online requires smarter solutions, such as better education, more resources for investigative units, and stronger international cooperation.

*On Encryption and Cybersecurity*

* Encryption is a technology that every person has the right to use;

* Introducing a backdoor in legal encryption software and algorithms to fight criminality is a futile and thoughtless concept;

*On Identity Verification and Biometrics*

* Any form of identity verification should be handled with extreme care;

* All persons should always have full autonomy over their genetic information, and

  medical records;

* Medical privacy should be strictly respected;

* All persons should be protected from groundless discrimination or stigmatisation

based on their race, sexuality, gender, sex, medical conditions, mental conditions or any other matters that are part of their nature;

*On International Agreements and Data Transfers*

* Governments need international cooperation to fight terrorism and global crime;

* Governments should only be authorised to share personal data in circumstances

  where it’s strictly necessary and proportional;

* Governments should be transparent with their data transfer conventions in accordance with public international agreements and legislation;

*On Accountability and Enforcement*

* Governments and private entities cannot be trusted to respect privacy or to handle sensitive data appropriately without proper legislation.

LYMEC calls for:

*On Big Data and Data Autonomy*

* The EU to collaborate with Internet standards bodies to develop a common data

  mobility standard and establish an EU-wide data processor registry, with compliance mandated for large data processors, to ensure individuals can effectively access, review, modify, and delete their personal data;

* The Council of Europe to pass a new Convention to strengthen individual privacy

  rights;

*On Chat Control and Surveillance*

* An unequivocal rejection of any legislation opening the door to chat control by  
  LYMEC, ALDE, and its Member Parties and Organisations;

* The LYMEC bureau to launch an awareness campaign to highlight the dangers of

  surveillance technology being weaponized against democratic movements and

  marginalized communities;

* LYMEC, ALDE, and its Member Parties and Organisations, to fight against any form

  of chat control or other disproportionate surveillance measures at every step of

  its possible introduction;

* LYMEC and ALDE to campaign against the current CSAM Regulation proposal;

* The EU to prohibit any indiscriminate scanning of private communication by any

  public or private institution;

* A lean oversight structure using existing European Data Protection bodies rather

  than new institutions or permanent taskforces;

* ALDE-PACE to pursue an unequivocal rejection of indiscriminate Chat Control and

  Surveillance;

* The Council of Europe to pass a new Convention to prohibit any indiscriminate

  Chat Control and Surveillance;

*On Encryption and Cybersecurity*

* The EU to introduce comprehensive legislation to protect encryption technology

  and its use, and to fund research in new methods proofed against possible quantum computing breakthroughs;

* The EU to prohibit Member States mandating backdoors in encryptions;

*On Identity Verification and Biometrics*

* LYMEC, ALDE, and its Member Parties and Organisations, to fight against draconian Identity Verification legislation or schemes;

* The EU to introduce comprehensive legislation to protect personal data such as,

  but not limited to: identifying information, genetic information, medical records, medical conditions, mental conditions, or any other matters concerning

  to one’s nature;

* The EU to prohibit Member States introducing mandatory identity verification schemes on websites;

*On International Agreements and Data Transfers*

* The EU to introduce comprehensive legislation on personal data transfers to foreign entities;  
* The EU to prohibit personal data transfers to foreign entities without due cause;  
* The EU to homogenise and standardise personal data transfer regulations and

  agreements across its member states and to foreign entities;

* The EU to reconsider its PNR and other international agreements on personal data

  transfers;

*On Accountability and Enforcement*

* The Court of Justice of the European Union to hold European governments and the

  European Commission accountable, including through prosecution, if citizens’ or

  allied government officials’ communications are intercepted in any manner

  inconsistent with current law and agreements;

* The EU to introduce legislation against companies and governments violating privacy laws with meaningful penalties, including fines scaled to revenue and

  criminal liability for egregious or repeated violations
