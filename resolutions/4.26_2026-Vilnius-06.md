Considering that:

- The escalation of the Russian aggression poses a threat to all of Europe.
- Reducing external dependencies within the EU strengthens its military position and strategic autonomy.
- Pooling demand gives EU countries greater market leverage when negotiating with major defense contractors.
- The fragmentation of European defence procurement and industrial capabilities leads to unnecessary duplication, higher costs, and limited economies of scale across the EU.
- The European Defence Technological and Industrial Base remains unevenly distributed among Member States, making cross-border cooperation and the inclusion of small and medium-sized enterprises essential for strengthening Europe’s defence resilience and supply chains.
- Major European collaborative programmes such as the A400M and Eurofighter have strengthened Europe’s technological and industrial capabilities and created cross-border industrial cooperation, while also experiencing significant delays and cost overruns linked in part to differing national requirements and complex multinational programme management.
- The interoperability of European armed forces is hindered by differences in technical standards, military requirements and procurement procedures, limiting the effectiveness of joint European defence capabilities.

Believing that:

- In times of crisis, cooperation and unity of European states is key to sustaining peace and prosperity within the region.
- Liberal democracies must act decisively to deter authoritarian regimes from violating territorial integrity and international law, as failing to do so would erode the values that bind Europe together.
- Joint rearmament programs such as SAFE bring European countries together in an effort to enhance our defence capabilities in order to deter Russia and any other external threats.
- European countries must be capable of developing European-made defence frameworks instead of depending on foreign aid and foreign military programs.
- European defence has to be made in Europe whenever it is possible.
- Europe should move towards becoming self-sustainable in terms of producing military defence capabilities.
- Joint rearmament projects enforce the necessity of cooperation between different EU member-states, thus reducing the threat of intra-EU frictions in decades to come.

LYMEC Calls For:

- The establishment of a functional, coordinated EU Defense Joint Venture framework, operating along the lines of the ReArm Europe Plan, and aimed at the acceleration of joint development and production of the most critical military components by the EU member states.
- The acceleration (and, wherever it is possible, expansion) of the ongoing EU cross-border defense projects, particularly within the spheres of drone and counter-drone systems, air and missile defense, cyber defense, and long-range precision strike capabilities - expanding on the already-existing frameworks such as the EDPCI and PESCO.
- Bolstering the European Defense Technological and Industrial Base by promoting new cross-border industrial partnerships, supporting smaller and medium-sized enterprises, encouraging joint European research and development, with a particular focus on dual-use technologies that serve both civilian and military purposes, and ensuring a network of strong, resilient European supply chains.
- The drive towards a unified, EU-wide approach to defense and industrial cooperation, manifested by the harmonization of technical and procurement standards, military requirements, and common capability priorities, with the goal of achieving interoperability between the EU armed forces and reducing the unnecessary area of overlap between their national defense programmes.
- The incorporation of a permanent, EU-level mechanism aimed at the monitoring and coordination of the EU defense joint venture projects, as well as ensuring transparency and implementation of deadlines, regularly reporting to the European Parliament.
- The strengthening of defence industrial cooperation between the EU and Ukraine through joint research, development, production and procurement, particularly in drone and counter-drone technologies, building on Ukraine’s battlefield experience and innovation, while further integrating Ukrainian defence companies into European defence industrial projects and supply chains.
