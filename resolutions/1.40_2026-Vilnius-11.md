Considering that:

- Article 2 of the Treaty on European Union establishes respect for human dignity, freedom, democracy, equality, the rule of law and human rights as fundamental values on which the European Union is founded;
- the European Commission assesses the rule of law in Member States through four key areas: the justice system, the anti-corruption framework, media freedom and institutional checks and balances;
- LYMEC has previously called for liberal and democratic actors throughout Europe to remain vigilant regarding early signs of democratic backsliding, and has affirmed that European institutions have a responsibility to actively defend the values enshrined in the Treaties (Policy Book - 1.35 – “European funds for European values!”);

Remembering that:

- the Economist Intelligence Unit’s 2024 Democracy Index downgraded Romania from a “flawed democracy” to a “hybrid regime”, following a decline in Romania’s overall democracy score from 6.45 to 5.99, citing deterioration in electoral processes, the functioning of government, public trust and corruption;
- the European Commission’s 2026 Rule of Law Report continued to identify challenges in Romania concerning, among others, judicial independence, the effectiveness of anti-corruption measures, media independence, and the transparency and predictability of the legislative process;
- in August 2026, the Romanian Parliament adopted legislation removing Dominic Fritz, the democratically elected Mayor of Timișoara and leader of Romania’s main reformist party USR, from office. This legislation applied a new legal consequence to a pre-existing situation, in breach of the prohibition of retroactive legislation under Romanian constitutional law and the EU principle of legal certainty, but the Romanian Constitutional Court nevertheless upheld the measure;
- Dominic Fritz was removed from office based on an administrative conflict of interest for which no concrete financial damage was established rather than a criminal conviction or finding of corruption. This conduct dated November 2020, during the first days of his first term as Mayor of Timișoara, when, instructed by municipal officials, he signed a document concerning a pre-existing urban-planning project whose documentation had been prepared before he took office and had already been signed by his predecessor.

Noting that:

- democratic backsliding rarely occurs through a single institutional change, but can develop through a series of measures which gradually erode checks and balances, judicial independence, political pluralism, institutional accountability and, ultimately, the foundations of liberal democracy;
- the weakening of democratic safeguards can occur through formally legal procedures when such procedures are used to undermine institutional independence, restrict political competition or reduce the ability of elected representatives and independent institutions to exercise their mandates.

Believing that:

- the continued recurrence of rule-of-law concerns in Romania and other EU Member States, despite successive monitoring and recommendations by European institutions, demonstrates the limitations of a system that relies primarily on dialogue and recommendations without sufficiently effective enforcement mechanisms;
- any political use of the justice system, including the removal of political opponents from office, as it has happened in Romania, is unacceptable and must never be tolerated within the EU. Wherever attempted by Member States, such actions must be prevented or appropriately sanctioned;
- the European Union cannot fully safeguard its democratic foundations when Member States are permitted to progressively abandon the principles of liberal democracy while retaining the benefits and decision-making rights of Union membership;
- early signs of democratic backsliding must be addressed promptly, before they become systemic;
- independent courts and constitutional institutions are essential safeguards of democracy only when their independence, transparency and accountability remain protected from political interference;

Local democracy and local self-government are integral parts of European democracy. National oversight and institutional cooperation are essential, but must not be misused through arbitrary interventions or retroactive legislation to undermine the autonomy of democratically elected local authorities.

- anti-corruption and integrity legislation is essential to liberal democracy, but its credibility depends on transparent, proportionate and politically neutral enforcement.

LYMEC calls for:

- European institutions to make full and consistent use of the legal instruments already at their disposal to address democratic backsliding, strengthen the rule of law, and protect fundamental rights across all Member States;
- EU institutions to develop stronger early-warning mechanisms allowing potential rule-of-law violations to be addressed before they become systemic;
- Member States to work towards a new European treaty that establishes binding non-regression standards, proportionate enforcement mechanisms subject to judicial review, and permanent rule-of-law safeguards;
- the European Commission to establish clear benchmarks and deadlines for addressing persistent rule-of-law deficiencies identified in its annual Rule of Law Reports, and to make greater use of infringement proceedings where national legislation or administrative practice undermine judicial independence, legal certainty, democratic participation or other rights protected by EU law;
- EU institutions and Member States to include robust and legally enforceable rule-of-law safeguards in future EU accession treaties, allowing for proportionate corrective measures in cases of serious democratic backsliding after accession, based on clear and objective criteria and subject to judicial review;
- ALDE Party, Renew Europe and LYMEC Member Organisations to continue to address rule-of-law concerns in all Member States, regardless of the political affiliation of the governments concerned.
