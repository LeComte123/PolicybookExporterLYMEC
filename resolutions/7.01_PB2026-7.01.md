Considering that:

* The fishing industry plays a vital role in the European economy, providing livelihoods for countless communities across the continent;

  * Overfishing and unsustainable fishing lead to a negative effect on marine ecosystems, leading to declines in fish populations and biodiversity loss;  
    * Climate change is exacerbating the challenges facing the fishing industry, with rising sea temperatures and ocean acidification affecting fish distribution and behaviour;  
    * The European Union committed itself to international agreements, such as the UN Sustainable Development Goal 14, which calls for the conservation and sustainable use of oceans, seas, and marine resources;  
    * Sustainable fishing practices can contribute to climate resilience, mitigate environmental impact, and protect marine life critical to livelihoods.

Recognising that:

* In opposition to research from the International Commission of the Exploitation of the Sea (ICES)\[1\] and Wageningen University\[2\] among others, The European Union has forbidden the use of pulse-trawling in 2019, making the only viable alternative heavy beam trawls which are known to inflict damage to the fish and benthic invertebrates in the path of the trawl due to the combined effects of tickler chains, a large amount of hard debris retained and high towing speed\[3\] \[4\];  
  * Pulse trawling reduces the unwanted bycatch of bottom life and undersized fish by more than 50%\[5\], offering a more targeted and sustainable approach than traditional trawling methods;  
    * The physical damage to the seabed per fishing hour has been reduced by approximately 20% when applying the pulse trawling method due to the lower fishing speed of pulse fishing\[6\];  
    * Pulse trawling provides for a lower penetration of the fishing gear into the Seabed\[7\];  
    * The use of pulse fishing has increased the survival of juvenile species like soles and plaice\[8\].

Believing that:

* A sustainable and resilient fishing industry is essential for the long-term health of European marine ecosystems and the well-being of coastal communities;  
  * Innovation and technological advancements are crucial for developing sustainable fishing practices and adapting to the challenges posed by climate change;  
    * Electric pulse fishing offers a more sustainable alternative to traditional fishing methods, such as the heavy beam trawling method, as it can reduce bycatch and minimise damage to marine habitats;  
    * Cooperation between the EU, International Organizations, and scientific bodies is essential to create and enforce policies that maintain marine biodiversity, regulate fish stocks, and ensure sustainable fishing practices.

LYMEC calls for:

* The legalisation of sustainable fishing techniques that reduce the negative impact of fishing on the environment, such as but not limited to electric pulse fishing;  
  * Increased investment in research and development to support the development of innovative fishing technologies that can reduce environmental impact, preserve and restore biodiversity and improve the sustainability of the industry;  
    * The elimination of unnecessary subsidies and market regulation to the fishing industry in general, but especially to the subsidies that have kept the outdated heavy beam trawl industry alive and prevented sustainable innovation in the past.
