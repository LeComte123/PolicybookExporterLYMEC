Cosignatories: NEO.DEPA (Cyprus), Alliance Youth (Northern Ireland), Mladi Pokreta slobodnih građana (Serbia), Momentum TizenX (Hungary), Young Liberals (UK), Attīstībai Youth (Latvia), Arthur Wu (IMS), Silvia Fernández (IMs), Nowoczesna Youth (Poland), For Youth\! (Latvia), USR Tineret, Junge liberale NEOS (Austria), Project NEON (Greece)

This resolution is an addition to the Policy Book Noting:

* That in April 2025 Spain and Portugal suffered a nationwide blackout, leaving millions without power, disrupting healthcare, transport, telecommunications and economic activity.

* That the blackout revealed the vulnerability of national energy systems with insufficient interconnections with neighbouring countries, as well as the lack of proper planning in energy production to support the grid, ensuring that the deployment of renewable sources does not jeopardise the stability of the system.

* That energy security and resilience are essential not only for national economies but for the stability and credibility of the entire European Union.

Considering that:

* The Iberian Peninsula remains an “energy island”, with an interconnection capacity

of around 3%, far below the EU target of 15 % by 2030\.

* The REPowerEU plan (2022) identifies boosting interconnection of European electricity networks as a key pillar for achieving climate neutrality, energy independence, and crisis resilience.

* The TEN-E Regulation (Trans-European Networks for Energy) and the Connecting Europe

Facility (CEF)provide the EU legal and financial framework to support Projects of Common Interest (PCIs), such as the Bay of Biscay subsea interconnection between Spain and France.

* The political family of Renew Europe and ALDE has consistently defended European

energy autonomy, cross-border interconnections, and the acceleration of renewable

deployment, stressing that solidarity in energy is a cornerstone of European integration.

LYMEC urges:

1. The European Commission and Member States to accelerate the implementation of

   electricity interconnection projects, particularly those affecting the Iberian Peninsula (Spain-France, Spain-Portugal, Spain-Italy), and other energy islands

   like Ireland, in order to reach at least 15 % interconnection capacity by 2030\.

2. The allocation of greater financial resources through the Connecting Europe

   Facility (CEF) and REPowerEU chapters of the Recovery and Resilience Plans to

   priority interconnection projects.

3. The establishment of enhanced cooperation mechanisms between Transmission System

Operators (TSOs) to streamline permitting, harmonise regulation, and avoid delays.

4. The development of energy solidarity mechanisms ensuring that, in case of future  
   blackouts or crises, neighbouring countries can supply electricity, provide storage capacities, and assist in the black-start (restarting) of the grid in extreme situations.  
5. The promotion of green hydrogen corridors and large-scale storage systems under  
   the TEN-E framework, as complementary infrastructure to strengthen Europe’s  
   energy independence and sustainability.

6. LYMEC further stresses that central and corridor countries such as Germany and

   France, due to their geographical position and role as key transit hubs, bear a

   particular responsibility to maintain above-average interconnection capacity and

   to facilitate the flow of electricity between peripheral regions of Europe, thereby ensuring a more resilient and integrated continental grid.
