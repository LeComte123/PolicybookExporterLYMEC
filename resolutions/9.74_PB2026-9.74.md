(Passed Spring 2025\)

*Noting that:*

* In recent years, global political instability, economic hardships, and humanitarian crises have increased, exacerbating challenges for vulnerable populations.

* The growing risks posed by novel security threats such as climate breakdown, disease outbreaks, and global famine are not going away and cannot be tackled through conventional military means alone.  
* The temporary suspension of foreign aid by the United States has further strained humanitarian efforts and weakened support for civil society organizations working to protect human rights and democratic values.

* American withdrawal and the shutting down of USAID will have dire consequences for many countries around the world, particularly in the health sector.

* Development Cooperation presents one of the strongest tools to exercise soft power.

* The dismantling of aid structures occurs rapidly, while rebuilding them is a long and challenging process.

* Some countries are particularly vulnerable due to prolonged conflict, political instability, and ongoing humanitarian crises, making external aid crucial for grassroots organizations, refugee support systems, and post-conflict reconstruction.  
* Without sustainable financial support, civil society organizations in crisis-affected nations risk collapse, threatening the protection of fundamental freedoms and human rights.  
* Hostile state governments, such as Russia and China, have used gaps in international development funding as opportunities to build ties with developing nations and exploit their resources.

*Considering that:*

* The EU has a long-standing commitment to promoting democracy, human rights, and development aid worldwide.

* There is growing recognition that international development funding has an important security dimension by tackling emerging risks and novel security threats  
* The reduction of aid from individual member states has created an urgent need for the EU to step up and fill the gap.  
* The EU has the capacity to compensate for national aid reductions and reinforce its role as a global leader in humanitarian and development assistance.  
* Local organizations are often best placed to ensure aid is effectively utilized and directly benefits those in need.  
* A long-term and predictable approach to aid is necessary to allow organizations to plan and implement sustainable development initiatives.  
* Any gap in international development funding which allows Russia or China to gain a foothold in key geopolitical regions is a threat to the security interests of the EU and European states.

*Believing that:*

* The EU must acknowledge the significant role of development aid in stabilizing regions affected by conflict, poverty, and human rights violations, as well as the role it plays in preventing future security threats.

* The reduction of aid contributions from individual member states should be counterbalanced by a stronger collective effort at the EU level.  
* Targeted support for local organizations ensures that financial resources reach those most in need and strengthens resilience in fragile regions.  
* EU aid efforts must prioritise those nations where the withdrawal of support from individual countries has left crucial civil society organizations and humanitarian programs in jeopardy.  
* Strengthening the EU’s global leadership in aid policies is crucial to upholding commitments to human rights, peace, and democracy.  
* EU allies in geopolitical regions which benefit from international development funding can have a key role in supporting the delivery of aid and tackling novel security threats, and European states must take opportunities to build relationships with these allies as equal partners  
* Whilst increases to defence spending are necessary, these increases should not be funded through cuts to international development aid as this limits Europe's ability to address novel security threats and prevent future security challenges.

*We therefore call for:*

* The EU and partner European states to increase funding for international aid to compensate for national reductions and prevent the deterioration of human rights conditions in affected regions.

* EU member states to devote at least 0.7 percent of their GDP for development cooperation, in line with the UN target for developed economies.

* The EU and partner European states to prioritize financial support for local organizations working on the ground in authoritarian regimes and conflict zones.

* The EU and partner European states to allocate sustained and increased financial support to countries where the withdrawal of aid has left a dangerous gap in humanitarian assistance and democratic rebuilding efforts.

* The EU and European partner states to adopt a long-term strategy for humanitarian and development assistance, ensuring stable and predictable commitments.  
* The EU and partner European states to find alternate ways to fund increases in defence spending instead of cutting overseas development funding  
* The EU to reinforce its global leadership in aid policies, ensuring that vital humanitarian efforts are not compromised by reductions in bilateral aid contributions.  
* The EU to carefully ensure that its development cooperation and international partnerships are subject to strict policy standards in line with the EU's

Common Security and Defence Policy, hence minimizing the danger of wasteful spending and spending that disregards the EU´s global interests
