LYMEC notes that:

* Space systems underpin Europe’s economy and security and are increasingly targeted by jamming, cyberattacks, and counterspace capabilities.  
* Europe’s access-to-space is strategically constrained by a very low orbital launch cadence compared to the US and China weakening resilience, learning curves, and competitiveness.  
* Orbital congestion and debris are a systemic security risk; destructive actions in orbit can create long-lived debris that endangers all operators and services.  
* International law already sets a clear red line against nuclear weapons / weapons of mass destruction in space.  
* Global consensus-building is active but fragile: the UN general assembly has called for ending destructive direct-ascent anti-satellite missile tests, and recent UN-level diplomacy shows how contested enforcement and verification remain.  
* The “race to the Moon” is accelerating into an infrastructure-and-standards contest with competing blocs seeking partners and influence.  
* Europe has credible building blocks for lunar autonomy and a lunar economy, yet political mandate and sustained funding determine speed and scale.  
* The EU is pursuing a sovereign connectivity constellation to provide secure communications and broadband services \- an important step for strategic autonomy if it is implemented competitively and at scale.

LYMEC believes that:

* A war in space would be catastrophic for everyone \- especially highly digitized societies.  
* The nuclear taboo in orbit must be strengthened with credible norms, transparency, and verification pathways.  
* Space security must combine rules-based governance with resilience  
* Europe’s strategic autonomy requires: reliable, affordable launch capacity at high cadence, open, easy-to-use connectivity and data infrastructure, and a regulatory environment that enables NewSpace to scale across Europe.  
* The Moon should not become a sphere of coercion or exclusion. Resource utilisation should be governed so that it is peaceful, transparent, and compatible with the Outer Space Treaty.  
* The lunar economy could benefit from commercial space flight. US companies like SpaceX, Blue Origin and Virgin Galactic currently dominate the sector with reusable rockets, satellite services and space tourism. It offers great opportunities for research and engineering, with an income stream of individual tickets that run into the millions.  
* For speed and scale, commercial space flights can create a needed influx of funds.  
* The EU should lead a coalition of responsible “space countries” to protect the orbital environment against destabilizing actors and incentives  
* A flagship international science project can be a stabilizer and confidence-building instrument, provided it is paired with hard security measures and clear participation conditions.

LYMEC calls for:

1) Preventing war in space  
* Champion an EU-led “Keep Space Peaceful & Usable” agenda  
* Make the ban on Weapons of Mass Destruction in orbit a politically enforceable red line. The EU needs to push for verification concepts, transparency measures, and coordinated consequences for violations.  
* Turn the UN general assembly call to end destructive anti-satellite testing into a wider binding norm.

2\) Increasing Europe’s launch cadence and autonomy

* A European “Cadence First” industrial plan: scale launch production, streamline range operations, enable rapid licensing, and procure launch services in a way that rewards reliability and flight rate (not only legacy structures).  
* Consider commercial space flight as a source to fund an increased European launch cadence and autonomy.  
* Establish EU-level rapid reconstitution capability (launch \+ satellite buses \+ terminals) for crisis replacement of critical services.

3\) Modernizing and speeding up ESA

* Reform ESA procurement and program governance to increase tempo: more milestone-based contracting, and clearer prioritization toward infrastructure platforms that unlock markets.  
* Back a clear political mandate and budget line for Europe’s launch to the Moon via ESA capabilities.  
* Make full participation in European space capability-building the default: support pathways for every EU country to engage meaningfully with ESA programs and European space industrial bases. EU funding needs to be aligned to incentivize reduced fragmentation.

4\) A credible European Moon \- Mars pathway

* Commit to “Europe on the Moon” infrastructure: support the Moonlight programme as a European anchor in lunar comms/nav services and interoperability with allied standards.  
* Build a European position on lunar resources to support lawful, transparent resource utilisation under clear governance, aligned with free access and due regard principles.  
* Keep Mars as a long-term objective: expand international cooperation for deep-space tech and habitation research, with Europe owning critical enabling layers.

5\) Space traffic management and debris as security policy

* Advance EU-wide space traffic management (STM) and resilience rules, aligned with the EU approach to STM and the EU’s security/defence space strategy.  
* Establishing a Space Debris Trade System (SDTS), whereby a pollution permit will be required for every kilogramme of waste in space. In order to limit space debris, a maximum permissible level of space littering should be set. This cap must be based on what scientists and engineers consider to be the maximum permissible amount for a still controllable risk of collisions with debris. The SDTS certificate must be available at the time of satellite launch in order to cover the potential debris at all times. When space debris is removed, new certificates for the debris remover are created in the same amount.

6\) Growing a European NewSpace ecosystem

* Push for national and European legal clarity for private space activity so startups can scale and invest with confidence.  
* Expand European space startup funding and procurement pathways to increase competition, innovation, and security of supply.  
* Pursue an ISS-like new multinational space station framework as a confidence-building, scientific, and industrial project among major spacefaring democracies.  
* Implement a performant IRIS² with startup-grade accessibility: standardized interfaces, transparent pricing, simple onboarding/procurement, and open service models so that SMEs and startups can build on it easily.  
* Establishing a network of Competence Hubs throughout the EU with simplified tax regimes. NewSpace founders should be able to set up their businesses easily and without red tape and be exempted from bureaucratic obligations as far as possible.
