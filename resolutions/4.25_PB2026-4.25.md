**Passed Spring 2026**

Venstres Ungdom, Ze\!Molodizhka, Radikal Ungdom, Young Liberals, GSM, Centerstudenter, AY, Joventut Nacionalista de Catalunya

Considering that:

On the Single Market and Fragmentation

* The European Single Market remains fragmented across 27 different national legal systems, particularly concerning corporate law, insolvency, and labour regulations;

* This "patchwork" of regulations imposes a "fragmentation tax" on startups and SMEs, costing European businesses billions in legal and administrative overhead;

* Unlike the United States, where a company can scale across 50 states under relatively uniform commercial principles, European companies must navigate 27 distinct jurisdictions to reach a similar scale;

* This fragmentation is equivalent to an implicit tariff of 45% on goods and 110% on services, making internal rulebook discrepancies a greater obstacle than external tariffs;

* The EU adopted over 13,000 new legislative acts between 2019 and 2024, compared to approximately 3,500 in the United States, creating a burdensome environment that hampers the ability of firms to scale;

* The current complexity forces many of Europe’s most promising scale-ups to relocate to the US to access simpler regulatory environments and deeper capital pools;

* Excessive administrative burdens and slow, non-digital public services continue to impose significant costs on businesses, particularly SMEs, limiting their ability to scale across the Single Market.

On the Draghi and Letta Reports

* Mario Draghi’s report on “The Future of European Competitiveness” \[1\] highlights a slow economic decline if the EU fails to radically simplify its regulatory environment and increase investment and to address its worsening competitiveness gap driven by high energy costs and fragmented capital markets;

* Letta’s report on the Single Market \[2\] explicitly advocates for a "28th Regime": a unified EU-wide legal framework for companies to allow businesses to operate cross-border by default;

* The EU’s share of global GDP is shrinking, and the productivity gap between the EU and the US is widening primarily due to the lack of an integrated tech and capital ecosystem;

* The EU's public research programme Horizon, with a budget of €100 billion, disperses resources across too many projects and lacks the commercial focus required to generate frontier innovation, while the EU's primary frontier research body, the European Innovation Council, operates with a budget of only €256 million–approximately 23 times less than its American counterpart DARPA

On Capital Markets and Investment

* The Capital Markets Union (CMU) remains unfinished, leaving European companies over-reliant on bank lending rather than equity markets;

* European private savings (approximately €33 trillion) are often funnelled into US markets because the European capital market is too fragmented to offer competitive returns or scale;

* Integration of capital markets is impossible without a standardized corporate form.

* Shifting towards funding a larger part of European pension systems would free up large amounts of money long term, generate innovation and ease up the burden of the working age. This being in contrast to the pay-as-you-go or unfunded system that remains as it does not have positive effects on innovation, nor can such a system be considered fair.

* The European Central Bank has estimated that if Europeans saved proportionally as much in equities and investment funds as Americans do, an additional eight trillion euros would be made available to European capital markets – approximately ten times more than the additional public investment Draghi identifies as necessary

Noting that:

On Existing Frameworks

* The Societas Europaea (SE) exists but is largely utilized by massive corporations due to its high capital requirements and complexity, leaving startups and SMEs underserved;

* The EU has successfully implemented "28th regimes" in other areas, such as the European Unitary Patent, demonstrating that optional, parallel EU-wide legal tracks are viable and effective;

* Previous attempts at a "European Private Company" (SPE) statute failed due to political disagreements over labour representation and tax headquarters;

* The Nordic model, with its focus on flexible labour markets, strong institutions, and ambitious research investment, provides a successful blueprint for economic dynamism;

* Sweden has achieved the highest number of stock exchange listings in Europe over the last decade, with the Swedish “investment and savings account” making retail investment accessible and simple;

* European defence procurement remains deeply fragmented along national lines, preventing the economies of scale and standardization necessary for a credible common defence industrial base;

On the Global Competitive Landscape

* Global competitors utilize industrial policies that leverage vast, unified domestic markets;

* The "Green Deal" requires a regulatory backbone that allows green-tech companies to scale at the speed of the climate crisis;

* AI and digitalization in general require increasingly large datasets and cross-border collaboration that are easily hindered by national data and corporate silos;

* A competitive European economy depends on a significantly reduced regulatory burden and the full digitalisation of administrative processes.

Believing that:

On Strategic Autonomy and Innovation

* European "Strategic Autonomy" is a fantasy without economic strength and a thriving private sector;

* The "EU-Inc" (or 28th Regime) should be a fully digital, "born-global" corporate identity that can be registered in hours, not months;

* Full implementation, rather than a modular or "opt-in" patchwork for Member States, is the only way to ensure the legal certainty required by venture capitalists and founders;

* A 28th Regime does not replace national traditions but provides a competitive alternative for those who wish to operate at a continental scale;

On Capital Markets

* The core competitiveness problem is an institutional failure to mobilize private capital and achieve genuine harmonization, rather than a lack of public investment;

* A more open European defence market, including a European defence innovation agency modelled on DARPA, would accelerate technological development and reduce duplicated costs;

LYMEC calls for:

On the Implementation of the 28th Regime

* The European Commission to propose a comprehensive "EU-Inc" (28th Regime) framework that provides a unified set of rules for company law, including simplified digital registration and reporting;

* This framework to be a Full Implementation across all Member States to prevent a ‘patchwork’ of 27 variations of the 28th regime, whilst respecting the principle of subsidiarity in its implementation without undermining the uniformity of the legal framework

* The EU-Inc to include a standardized Insolvency Code and a simplified Tax Base (BEFIT) specifically tailored for companies opting into this regime;

* Reform EU merger control guidelines to allow the European Commission's Directorate-General for Competition to take full account of global competitive dynamics and strategic industrial considerations when assessing mergers, rather than applying a purely European market definition that prevents the emergence of globally competitive European champions in strategic sectors.

On Capital Markets and Competitiveness

* The immediate acceleration of the Capital Markets Union (CMU), ensuring that an "EU-Inc" company can list on a unified European stock exchange without navigating 27 different sets of listing rules;

* Significantly strengthen the mandate of the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA) to act as a pan-European supervisor with binding authority comparable to the US Securities and Exchange Commission;

* Encourage member states to transition from pure pay-as-you-go pension systems towards funded second-pillar pension models, drawing on the Swedish, Danish and Dutch experience, with the aim of generating long-term institutional investment capital that can flow into European venture capital, infrastructure, and capital markets and promote intergenerational equity

* Establish a uniform European Individual Investment Account modelled on the Swedish ISK to channel more household savings into productive investments;

* Develop a European Venture Capital passport enabling funds to raise capital and invest freely across all Member States under a single authorization;

* The EU to implement the key recommendations of the Draghi Report, specifically the creation of a "Competitiveness Coordination Tool" to streamline regulation and cut "red tape" by 25% for SMEs;

* Create a unified European clearing and settlement infrastructure to replace the current patchwork of 295 national trading venues, clearing houses, and settlement systems, drastically reducing transaction costs, increasing visibility of European stocks, creating an environment where it's easier for companies to be introduced and increasing liquidity across European capital markets;

* Invest EU resources in developing a deep, liquid EU-level market that facilitates long-term green and innovation bonds, channelling private capital into the net-zero transition and digital transformation through market mechanisms rather than relying predominantly on public expenditure.

* The implementation of binding EU-level targets for administrative simplification, including fully digital public services and measurable reductions in compliance burdens for businesses.

On Talent and Labor

* The EU-Inc framework to include a "European Social Security Pass" to allow employees of 28th-regime companies to move between Member States without losing pension or healthcare continuity;

* A unified Stock Option Framework within EU-Inc to allow startups to attract global talent by offering equity that is taxed and treated uniformly across the Union;

Research and Innovation Policy Reform

* Transform the European Innovation Council (EIC) into a genuine European frontier research and defence technology agency – a European DARPA – with a budget of at least €3 billion annually, funded through reallocation within the Horizon programme, with a mandate explicitly focused on dual-use and defence-relevant breakthrough technologies.

* Reform the governance of EU research funding so that programme directors are selected from among leading researchers and industry innovators rather than EU officials, given multi-year mandates with full operational autonomy, and empowered to reallocate or terminate funding from underperforming projects without the bureaucratic constraints that currently undermine the commercial impact of EU research spending.
